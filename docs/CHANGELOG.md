# PERF-1 changelog

## A note on version numbers

**0.13.0 is the first public release.** Its code is identical to the last development build, 0.12.8.

**0.14.0 is the second.** Its code is identical to the last development build, 0.13.15; the builds 0.13.1 to 0.13.15 in the git history are its steps.

**0.15.0 is the third.** Its code is identical to the last development build, 0.14.21; the builds 0.14.1 to 0.14.21 are its steps.

**0.15.1 is a fix-up of 0.15.0.** Its code is identical to development build 0.15.4; the builds 0.15.1 to 0.15.4 in the git history are its steps (the public number reuses the first of them).

**0.15.2 is a second fix-up.** Its code is identical to development build 0.15.5.

**0.16.0 is the fourth release.** Its code is identical to development build 0.15.27; the builds 0.15.6 to 0.15.27 in the git history are its steps.

**0.16.1 is the fifth release.** Its firmware is identical to development build 0.16.4; the builds 0.16.1 to 0.16.4 in the git history are its steps (the public number reuses the first of them). The Pieces and Library pages (0.16.3 and 0.16.4) are in the source but not on the website yet: they come with 0.17.

**0.16.2 is the sixth release.** Its firmware is identical to development build 0.16.16; the builds 0.16.5 to 0.16.16 in the git history are its steps. Its first reason is a fix: M-VAVE's new M-UPGRADE (3.11) could not find PERF-1, so there was no way back to the stock firmware with it. The Library & pieces page is in the source but not on the website yet: it comes with 0.17, with the boot stub (0.16.17) and a MIDI CC for every setting (0.16.18), which are on `vis`.

**0.17.0 is the seventh release.** Its firmware is development build 0.16.29's code (it reports `FM-1_7001700`); the builds 0.16.17 to 0.16.29 in the git history are its steps. The Pieces page and the Library are on the website from this release, with the Library's relay live. The next development builds count from 0.17.1.

Before the first release the firmware was developed on two lines: a "main line" (0.3.x to 0.5.1) and an experimental `vis` branch that went through the numbers 0.4.0 to 0.12.8. The commit messages in the git history use those development numbers; they are all contained in 0.13.0. The leading 0 stays until PERF-1 reaches its first finished state, which will be 1.0.

The unit reports its version as `FM-1_7XXYYZZ` (0.13.0 is `FM-1_7001300`); the installer checks it.

## Next (development builds from 0.17.1)

- **Holds and the snare roll (0.17.91, the maker's soak on 0.17.90).** The snare roll with Land on went silent after about 20 minutes of play: it kept its place in beats since the start, and that far in a float could no longer count a sample's step. Pressed again and again, the silent rolls stacked up (about 5 % of the CPU each) until the sound dropped out (the log's 5, 16, 42 and 66 dropouts, all there). It counts within the beat now, and a roll, a riser or a rewind pressed again starts over in its own voice instead of adding one. HALFTIME held: back to the live mix on the bar (two beats or a beat at slow tempos and the slowest speed), keeping its speed; it started over off the grid when its 0.95 s of recording ran out, sliding down again. BRAKE and SPIN BK held: stopped, silent, until let go, then back on the grid; they repeated the last bit.
- **More resonance on the 303 (0.17.90, the maker, after the A/B).** Reso 90 now goes 96 % of the way to ringing (it was 91 %: about 6 dB more peak) and 100 rings; the level is the same. And a fix: changing the acid's filter type ran up to 31 samples of the old type's numbers in the new one (they share them), which could blow the acid up for a moment; now the new type starts at once, from rest.
- **The 303 filter is a diode ladder now (0.17.89, the maker: "it should do more").** FILTERS > Acid > 303 was a transistor ladder (Moog style), which measured within 1 dB and a little brighter than Low. Now it is the TB-303's own kind, a diode ladder (after Zavalishin, The Art of VA Filter Design 5.10): four stages that lean on their neighbours, solved without delay. Its slope is softer, its resonance rounder, and the passband thins as the resonance rises, as a 303's does; the 150 Hz high-pass in its feedback keeps the deep bass, and the soft clip on its input stays. As loud as before, a little darker, with the same cost. Also: the RETURNS page's first Duck setting reads **MIX** (it was KIT: the returns duck as the rest of the mix does), and the SNIP LAND page says what it times (the riser, the rewind and the snare roll).
- **Snips over a heavy piece (0.17.88, the maker's log: four snips with the BELL over HOUSE 01, one dropout).** The snips cost 14-24 % less (the voices' filter states flushed once a chunk, not each sample; what does not change within a chunk worked out once); the BELL plays a plain sine once its overtones have died (1.2 s) and ends at -40 dB (2.1 s, was 3 s). When the load is high, fewer snips sound at once: three at shed level 1, two from level 2 (the oldest gives way).
- **The install safety bench passed on the unit (2026-11-21, no firmware change).** The cable pulled and the power cut at each point of an install (while it checks, just after the loader starts, at a third and at nine tenths of the write, at the restart), back to the stock firmware and again, stores while playing, a backup and restore after a factory reset, and 40 starts in a row: each install completed, nothing was lost. The manual no longer calls the stopped-install protection experimental, and says what a cable pulled while the installer checks does (nothing written; the FM-1 waits dark until it is switched off and on).

(freesound.org/s/242663, CC0), from 0.103 s (no silence before it), as recorded and an octave down, at the snips' loudness. 3.9 KB of flash (the hoover model was 5.0). From ChatGPT's second look at 0.17.63.
- **The acid's Sync, PWM and Laser held across chunks (0.17.63).** The acid works out its slave's pitch (Sync), its width (PWM), Laser's pitch and the Wow drive every 32 samples, counted across chunks, but each chunk began from the defaults (ratio 1, width 0.5, no Wow) until the next update: up to 31 samples of the wrong pitch or width at every chunk's start, a faint buzz on those oscillators. - **Fewer audio chunks, so less load on the first core (0.17.62).** A render (two 256-frame chunks) was also cut at every MIDI clock pulse, 24 a beat, and every chunk costs the unit a fixed 0.8-1.3 ms whatever its length (the maker's TRANCE 01 logs: half the chunks were cut short). Only the step's pulse cuts a chunk now (its notes land on their sample, as before); the five pulses between steps send only the MIDI clock out, so each goes out at the nearer chunk edge, up to 2.9 ms early or late. The grid doesn't drift, and a follower steps on the step's pulse, which stays exact. On the host, TRANCE 01 renders in 20 % fewer chunks (2.74 to 2.20 a render); the notes are identical, loudness within 0.04 LU.
- **Song mode that sounds arranged (0.17.61, the 1.0 list).** A set-list slot with bars now hands over the way a track would. Into a slot that brings the drums in (or lifts the energy by 25 or more): a build over the last bars of the slot before (the genre's length, at most half the slot; if that slot had the drums out, they come in for the build), and the drop on the new slot's first bar, also when the new slot is another piece (a new piece restarted the engine's section, so its drop was lost: the engine's new `en_drop`). Into a slot without the drums: a breakdown, the groove letting go on the one, and it stays down through the next slot without drums. Anything else: the fill, as before. The engine's build can now come from the groove with its own length (`en_build_bars`). Test: `songarr.amb`.

What it is: app.bin's first 0xEE0 bytes (flash 0x4120..0x4FFF) are the stub, which the SPL enters on every boot; the app proper (the body) is linked from XIP 0x02001000 (flash 0x5000; app.ld: 0x8C000 at most, below the marker's sector 0x91000; PERF-1's is 436 KB). RAM: the stub owns 0x01C7C000..1F, NOINIT starts at 0x01C7C020; .data+.bss unchanged. Build: tools/build.py build_stub (Melodee's flags, the lock checked; a release refuses another stub). **The first install of 0.16.17 over an older PERF-1 rewrites sector 0x4000 once: a cut in those first moments is not protected** (16 of 1854 cut points in the model); every later install is (0 of 1837). Tests (Melodee's, ported): boot_stub_test (16143 checks), boot_stub_pkg_test (265), loader_powercut_test with boot_fixtures (a power cut after every erase and programmed page, each state given to the stub's own decision, then resumed to a bit-exact end), ota_test (the loader kept, the record cleared, a damaged staging erased, a refused update keeps it). **Not yet on hardware**: cold and warm boots, OCT- at power-on, a pulled cable and a power cut mid-install.

## 0.14.0 (the builds 0.13.1 .. 0.13.15 are its steps)

- **The xruns were a lost interrupt** (0.13.15): the audio interrupt cleared its flag after the render. A render that ran past its half (11.6 to 12.4 ms in a lofi piece at 55 % load) therefore cleared the flag the next half had already raised: that half was never rendered and its buffer played a second time. Each xrun in a 309 s log was exactly one render missing from the renders count. The flag is now cleared before the render, so a late render is followed at once by the next one and nothing is lost; `XR` counts only halves never rendered, `late` (new in the status log and the installer's Live status) counts renders that ran past their half. Load shedding follows: one step at a time (a single overrun used to climb s0 to s3 in under two seconds), on a lost half, or on six late renders in ten seconds. The installer's status CSV also records the slowest chunk of each window and the histogram of render times. On the unit: the same lofi piece, 9 xruns in 309 s before, 0 xruns in 642 s after (88 late renders, the longest 13.3 ms, absorbed).
- **Steps page** (0.13.1): a ratchet (x2, x3, x4, x6, flam, swells), a push (late, in twelfths of a step) and a level (ghost, soft, accent) for each step of each lane, stored with the piece in the record's spare bytes (older pieces load with none); some genres ship with tricks.
- **Bass line page** (0.13.2): the drone as notes (Root, Kick, Eighths, Off-beat, Walk, Riff) with Length, Move and Glide, a genre's own rhythm and riff; lofi, house, dubstep, electro, trap and the newer genres start as a bass line.

- **Feel page** (0.13.4): the genre's ghost notes, how much accents and ghosts count, the pad's comping rhythm (auto, hold, seven rhythms), humanize; per-genre comping, ghost steps and accents.
- **Six more genres** (0.13.5): disco, funk, reggae, synthwave, amapiano and trip-hop, sixteen in all, each with its own flash object.
- **Phrase page** (0.13.6): a chain of patterns by bar (nine presets, the Amen's 1 1 3 4 among them), eight slice permutations of the drum steps (and an automatic one), half and double time, and a condition for each drum lane (odd or even bars, first or last of four, first of eight, fills only, not in fills).

- **Dub replaces Jungle** (0.13.14): the fifth genre is now DUB (pieces V01-V10, 64-76 BPM): one-drop, steppers and rockers patterns, a deep bass on the one and three with long notes, the skank on the off-beats, a long dotted-quarter echo, a worn tape latched on, a minor one-four vamp, and a breakdown that keeps the kick. Jungle's heavy ratchet-and-chop drums cracked the first core in every measurement. Pieces stored in that genre's slot keep their old settings under the new name (reset the bank, or store them again, to hear Dub).
- **Sections shaped by genre** (0.13.13): the bar before a breakdown thins out (percussion, open hat and half the hats go) so the break is a release, not a cut; a breakdown keeps the kick in house, techno and dubstep and the hats in drum and bass, darkens the whole mix a little, and raises the pad less in trap, dubstep, lofi and trip-hop; a build is two bars in trap, lofi and trip-hop, eight in house, otherwise four, with the snare climbing a fifth, the open hat on every eighth in the last bar and, in the four-on-the-floor genres, the kick back before the drop; the drop's open hat is a crash that rings and the chord goes home most of the time; and drum fills are one of six (the old ramp and five new ones) instead of always the same snare roll.
