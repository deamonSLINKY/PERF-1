Manual

Printable: the full manual (PDF) · the quick manual (PDF, eight A4 pages) · the beginner's guide (PDF)

PERF-1 is free firmware that turns your M-VAVE FM-1 into a groovebox that plays by itself. When you switch it on, a piece of music starts. The piece has drums, a bass line, a pad, an acid line and a short plucked melody. PERF-1 makes up the music while it plays, so the music never repeats exactly. There are twenty-two styles, from techno and house to lofi, hip-hop, reggae, drum and bass, trance and ambient. Each style has ten pieces.

You do not have to program anything to start. You control the music. You change the chords, you take the beat out (a breakdown) and bring it back, you mute parts and you add effects. Or the auto-DJ can do these things for you. If you want to do more, you can edit almost everything, from the drum steps to the sounds. You can save each piece that you change.

The FM-1 also works as a USB audio and MIDI device. Thus, you can record what you play directly into your computer.

If you are new, read Start here below first. The numbered sections after it are the full reference.

PERF-1 is made by deamonSLINKY. It is free and open source (GPL-3.0-only). The credits (section 19) give the people whose work it uses. PERF-1 is still new and it changes frequently. Thus, keep a way to go back to the stock firmware, M-VAVE's own firmware (section 1 explains how).

Start here: your first hour

This guide is for your first hour. You do not need music theory, and you do not have to set up anything. Each step is one thing to press. Each step also tells you what you will hear.

If you want all the details, the quick manual shows every control on eight pages. The rest of this manual, from section 1, explains everything. This part is also a printable PDF.

What PERF-1 is

PERF-1 turns your M-VAVE FM-1 into a small band that plays by itself. When you switch it on, music starts. You hear drums, a bass, a soft pad, an "acid" line (a twisting line with a sharp, wet sound) and a plucked melody. PERF-1 makes up all of this music while it plays, so it never repeats exactly.

You control the band. You do not have to play notes. You change the chords. You take the beat out and bring it back. You add effects, you mute parts, and you keep the moments that you like.

Before you start

  • Try it before you install it. The emulator runs PERF-1 in your web browser, with sound. All the steps in this guide also work there.
  • To install, use the web installer in Chrome or Edge. It takes a couple of minutes. Use a USB cable that carries data. Do not use a cable that only charges.
  • You can always go back. The same installer page puts M-VAVE's own firmware back. Keep copies of the items that are important to you. When you change the firmware, you can lose the items that you saved on the stock firmware.

Your first five minutes

1. Switch it on and listen. After a short animation, a piece starts to play by itself. Turn MASTER to a comfortable volume. Nothing that you press in this guide can cause damage.

2. Tap EDIT. At the start of the next bar, the chord changes. The whole band follows: the bass, the pad and the melodies all change to fit the chord. Tap it a few more times.

3. Play a few keys. The keys play a melody over the band. Every key is a note of the music's scale, so no note is wrong. Play anything, slowly or quickly, and it fits. The chords and the scale of the piece do not change.

4. Turn PRESETS one click. A new piece starts, with a new key, a new tempo (speed) and new sounds. There are ten pieces in each style.

5. Hold GLO and turn PRESETS. Now you change the style. There are twenty-two styles: techno, lofi, house, dubstep, dub, drum and bass, breaks, garage, electro, trap, disco, funk, reggae, synthwave, amapiano, trip-hop, industrial, psytrance, dark forest, hip-hop, trance and ambient. When you find a style that you like, release GLO.

Make it move

6. Tap SAVE: a breakdown. At the next bar, the drums stop. The music becomes softer and more dreamy.

7. Tap SAVE again: the build and the drop. The drums come back and climb for a few bars. Then there is a short silence. Then all the parts play together at once. DJs like this moment very much. Tap SAVE, wait, and tap SAVE again. That is all.

8. Press FX: effects on the keys. Now each key is an effect. The screen shows the names of the effects.

  • Tap a key to keep its effect on.
  • Hold a key to use its effect only while you hold the key.
  • To stop an effect, tap the same key again. To stop all the effects at once, tap OCT-.
  • To leave, press FX again. Try the gate, the phaser, the tape and the beat repeats.

The Perform screen, a first look

9. Press HOME, then HOME again (do not press too quickly). This is the Perform screen. It shows the eight parts of the band and a mood bar.

10. Turn ALGORITHM, the knob that sets the mood. Turn it left for calm music with few notes. Turn it right for busy, bright music. The acid, the hats (the hi-hats), the melody, the bass and the reverb (the sound of a room) all change together.

11. Tap the keys to mute parts. On this screen, the keys turn the parts of the band off and on. The screen shows which key controls which part. Mute the kick drum and listen to the difference. Then turn it on again.

12. Let it play itself. Hold GLO and tap the F5 key (the second white key from the right). "DJ" comes on at the top of the screen. Now PERF-1 does the breakdowns, builds and drops by itself, like a DJ. To stop this, do the same again.

Keep what you like

13. Save the piece. If you changed a piece and you like it, press GLO, then SAVE. The screen shows that it stored the piece. The piece is there the next time that you switch on.

Each style keeps its own ten pieces.

To put a piece back to how it was at the start, go to GLO. Then hold SEL and SAVE together for a moment.

When you want more

You do not need these things to have fun, but they are there.

  • Turn SELECT to go through the pages. Each page has four knobs below the screen. The page name tells you what the knobs change: the pad, the bass, the acid, the drums, the reverb and much more.
  • The Jam page (turn SELECT past Global) sets what the keys play. It can be the pluck, the pad, the acid or the bass, as single notes or full chords. It also sets how high the notes are. On the pages of a part (the pad's, bass's, acid's or pluck's pages), the keys play that part.
  • Change the scale on the Global page (press GLO). On this page, each key adds a note to the music or removes it. ALGORITHM sets the mode of the scale (a brighter or darker minor, or a major). On all other pages, ALGORITHM sets the mood.
  • Hold HOME for the settings.
  • The SET page has an Intro. The Intro starts with only a pad. Then it brings in the band, one part at a time, over a few minutes. It is good for mixing in, or for playing over. To get there, hold HOME, then turn SELECT to SET.
  • The quick manual has eight pages. Print it and keep it next to the FM-1.

If something goes wrong

  • You do not know where you are? Press HOME.
  • An effect does not stop? Press FX, then OCT-, then FX again.
  • The music stopped? Press PLAY.
  • A piece sounds strange after many changes? Turn PRESETS to a different piece and then back. Or reset the piece: on the GLO page, hold SEL and SAVE.
  • You want the original firmware back? Go to the installer page. Use "Back to the stock firmware" at the bottom.
  • You found a bug (a problem) or you have an idea? Tell us on GitHub. Most of the newest features came from ideas like yours.

1Install

You can try PERF-1 before you install it. The PERF-1 emulator runs the same firmware in your web browser, with sound. Play it with the mouse, a touch screen or your computer keyboard.

Installing PERF-1

  1. Open the web installer in Chrome or Edge.
  2. Connect the FM-1 directly to the computer with a USB cable that carries data (some cables only charge). You do not need a hub, a probe or Transporter.
  3. Close all other programs that use the FM-1's MIDI port, for example your DAW (recording software).
  4. When the browser asks, allow MIDI with SysEx. SysEx is the type of MIDI message that the installer uses to send the firmware.
  5. Wait until the Install button is not grey (while it is grey, the page is still loading the firmware). The line below the button shows the version that it will write (for example, PERF-1 1.0.0). If that version is older than you expected, hard-refresh the page (Ctrl+F5, or Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac). Then the browser does not use an old saved copy.
  6. Press Install.

The bar and the line above it show each stage: verifying, switching to update mode, writing, restarting, done. Do not disconnect the cable or close the page while it writes. If you try to leave, the page asks you first. When the install is complete, the FM-1 restarts into PERF-1. Then the page checks that the FM-1 reports the correct version.

Install firmware at your own risk. The author accepts no responsibility if this custom firmware damages your FM-1 or makes it work incorrectly. Keep your own copies of the .syx files that you load. When you replace the firmware, you can lose all the items that you stored (pieces, settings, DX7 voices).

Going back to the stock firmware

The "Back to the stock firmware" part, at the bottom of the same page, puts M-VAVE's own FM-1 firmware, V15, back on the unit.

  1. Download the file that the page links to, FM-1.fwsc. It is also on m-vave.com/download. M-VAVE's link now gives V16. For V15, the page also links to the copy of M-VAVE's original file that the Internet Archive kept (V15). The page checks the fingerprint of each file before it writes anything.
  2. Select that file on the page. The page checks the SHA-256 fingerprint of the file (a code that identifies the exact file). It refuses all other files.
  3. Connect the FM-1. Then press Install the stock firmware.

This works from PERF-1, from safe mode and from update mode. M-VAVE's own updater, M-UPGRADE (Downloads, PC Software), also works from PERF-1 0.16.13 and later. M-UPGRADE 3.11 does not find older PERF-1 builds ("A USB MIDI device is present but did not answer"). For those builds, use the button on this page.

V16. M-UPGRADE now offers V16 (FM-1_016), the newer firmware from M-VAVE. From PERF-1 0.16.15 and later, you can go in either direction. M-UPGRADE changes PERF-1 directly to V16, and this page installs PERF-1 on an FM-1 that runs V16. The button on this page accepts the V15 file or the V16 file.

V16 changes the FM-1's boot head, the first 16 KB of its memory. PERF-1 lets that change occur only between M-VAVE's own V15 and V16 heads. With an older PERF-1, the FM-1 shows UPDATE FAILED and M-UPGRADE times out (error 258). In that case, select V15's FM-1.fwsc with Select firmware in M-UPGRADE, or use the button on this page. Then update the stock V15 to V16 with M-UPGRADE.

One program at a time. On Windows, only one program at a time can use the FM-1's MIDI port. This page and the Pieces page release the port 4 seconds after they last communicated with the FM-1. (Live status keeps the port while it runs.) Thus, M-UPGRADE or your music software can open the port while the page is still open. If a program says that the FM-1 is busy or "present but did not answer", close the other programs and pages that use the port. Then disconnect the FM-1 and connect it again.

Update mode

In update mode, the installer can write to the FM-1. To start update mode yourself, hold OCT- and OCT+ together for 5 seconds while PERF-1 runs.

  • After 2 seconds, the screen counts down: UPDATE MODE IN 3...
  • If you release the buttons before the end, the screen shows UPDATE MODE CANCELLED.
  • At 5 seconds, the FM-1 goes into update mode and shows UBOOT.

If an install stops before the end, the FM-1 stays in update mode until a write is complete. Press Install again. If the page says that it lost the FM-1, first disconnect the cable and connect it again.

Safe mode

If PERF-1 crashes or freezes two times in sequence while it starts, the FM-1 starts in safe mode instead. The screen is black and shows PERF-1 SAFE MODE, with the address of the crash at the bottom. There is no sound, but USB works. In safe mode, you can:

  • install again from the web installer, or
  • press PLAY to try to start PERF-1 again.

If the start still fails after this, the FM-1 goes into update mode automatically, ready for the installer.

If PERF-1 crashes while it runs, the next start tells you. The screen shows RESTARTED AFTER A CRASH and a PC address for two and a half seconds. If you report the crash, write down that address.

Recovering an FM-1 that won't start

An install that stopped before the end (PERF-1 0.16.17 and later). If the power or the cable is lost while PERF-1 installs, switch the FM-1 on again. The key lights flash three times and the screen stays dark. This means that the FM-1 waits for the installer. Then do these steps:

  1. Connect the FM-1.
  2. Open the Install page.
  3. Press Install again. The installer completes the install.

To get to this mode on purpose, hold OCT- alone while you switch the FM-1 on. If the lights give one long flash instead, the rescue was not possible. The FM-1 is then in the chip's own USB mode (below).

This function comes from ChoralRoot's boot stub (a small program that the FM-1 starts through), as Melodee uses it. It is experimental in PERF-1. The method is tested in Melodee and ChoralRoot, and PERF-1 starts through it at every start. But PERF-1's own copy has not been through a stopped install yet. Thus, do not rely on it on purpose.

The first install of 0.16.17 over an older PERF-1 has a short time, near the start, that is not protected yet. Keep the cable connected and the power on until the install is complete. If you install the stock firmware or a different firmware, that install removes the boot stub.

  • Covered: a bad PERF-1 install. The installer checks the package before it sends it. It checks each packet that it writes. After the restart, it reads back the FM-1's version. On the FM-1, there is safe mode, update mode after four failed starts, the OCT- + OCT+ hold, and a USB command that starts update mode. The installer works from all of them.
  • Not covered: a damaged start-up sector or boot loader (the part of the memory that starts the FM-1). Also not covered: a loss of power or cable during a write, before the installer has saved its record of where to continue. The protection for stopped writes comes from the design. It has not been tested with a real power cut. Nobody has found a hardware way in for this chip (test pads, a strap, a key combination). An FM-1 that fails in this way can be permanently lost.

If the FM-1 does not start but it still gets to the chip's own update mode (one long flash; it shows on USB as 4C4A:8057, UBOOT), use Melodee's recovery tool. It is the easiest way back on all computers. Download fm1-recover for Windows, Linux or Mac from the Melodee 1.4.0 release. You do not need Python. The tool puts M-VAVE's own V15 back (FM-1.fwsc from M-VAVE: the tool accepts only the unchanged file). Then install PERF-1 again from the web installer.

On Windows, the tool needs the WinUSB driver for that one device (4C4A:8057). Install the driver one time with Zadig. Do not change the driver of the FM-1's normal MIDI and audio device. First, run fm1-recover --info: it only reads, and it does not write. The tool keeps a full backup of the memory before it writes. It is Kerem Kilic's (Ellic Studio), from the Melodee project, and it is not part of PERF-1.

On a Mac, the PERF-1 source also has its own rescue tool, tools/fm1_rescue.py (and its wrapper fm1_rescue.sh). It puts the stock V15 back from a Mac. It uses sudo, pyusb and libusb-package. First, it makes a backup of the full memory. Then it writes only the 4 KiB sectors between 0x4000 and 0x93000 that are different from V15. Then it reads all of the memory back to check it. It accepts only the exact V15 file.

The tool was written for an FM-1 that crashes at each start. It uses the method of FM-1-transporter, which that project tested on real hardware. Use it only as a last resort. It is not a guarantee. In other cases, recovery needs FM-1-transporter or hardware that you possibly do not have.

2Getting started

Are you new to PERF-1? Start here, at the top of this manual, takes you through your first hour. This section is the short version.

  1. Switch it on. The deamonSLINKY card shows (the slinky mascot jumps twice and blinks, then the name and PRESENTS show). Then the PERF-1 card shows (a festival picture, the version, "by deamonSLINKY", GPL-3.0). On it, sixteen steps fill in and the white keys come on with them. Push any button or key to skip the cards. Then the music starts: the piece that you played last, or a HYPNO piece at random (SETTINGS, Start).
  2. Turn SELECT to go through the pages. The four knobs below the screen change what each page shows.
  3. Press SEQ for the drums. Hold it for the step sequencer (the drum beat as 16 steps). Then tap the white keys to change the beat.
  4. Play the keys. On the page of a voice, the keys play that voice over the music. On other pages, they play the voice of the Jam page. Turn PRESETS for a different piece. Hold GLO and turn PRESETS for a different genre. On the page of a voice, hold SEL and turn ALGORITHM to hear the other sounds of that voice.
  5. Do you want to play the bass yourself? Press LFO for the bass page. Then hold SEL and press ENV. The bass stops its own part, and the keys play the bass instead. To give the part back to the bass, press SEL + ENV again. The JAM page (turn SELECT past Global) is a keyboard that never changes the scale.
  6. Tap SAVE for a breakdown on the next bar. Tap it again for a build and then the drop. A build is four bars long. It is two bars in lofi, trip-hop, hip-hop and ambient. It is eight bars in house, dubstep, DnB, psytrance and trance.
  7. Press FX for the effects. Tap a key to latch its effect on (to keep it on). Or hold the key to play the effect only while you hold it.
  8. HOME goes to the Tempo page. Press HOME again, not too quickly, for the Perform screen. Press it twice quickly for the visualizers. Hold it for the settings.
  9. Do you like a piece? Go to Global (GLO) and press SAVE to store it. Storing works in every genre: a piece that you store in lofi or house stays there.

4The screen

The screen has five bands. From top to bottom, they are: the header, the chord, the five voices, the page tabs and the knobs. Every colour theme uses this layout (its name is Console). The themes change only its colours.

The header, left. This part shows the tag of the piece. The tag is the number of the piece, with a letter for the genre (01 for HYPNO, L03, H07, D02, V01 for dub, N05 for DnB, B04, G09, E06, T10, S03, F01, R07, W01, A05, P02, I04, Y01, O08, M06, U03, Z02 for the others; the table in section 6 has them all). A mark in the tag shows that the piece has changes that you did not store. The root and the mode are next to the tag.

The header, right. This part shows the tempo. When an outside MIDI clock (another device that sends its tempo over MIDI) controls the tempo, the tempo shows EXT. When PERF-1 is stopped, a play triangle shows next to the tempo. On battery, a cell shows the charge. When USB supplies power to the FM-1, the cell has a bolt in it. The slinky imp (a small figure) shows here for a moment at a drop, a breakdown or a build. To the left, marks show what is on:

The five voices. Each voice has a strip: the pad, bass, acid, pluck and drums, in their colours. At the bottom of each strip is the sound of the voice (on the drums, the kit). A meter shows its level.

  • The bass, acid and pluck show the notes of the bar. The notes fall to the playhead. Higher notes are higher on the screen, and accents are bigger. The slides of the acid are joined. A held drone is a long bar. The pad shows its held voices. The drums show their lanes.
  • On the pages of a voice, its strip opens wide. It shows a picture of what the voice plays: the pad's notes on two octaves of keys, the bass, acid and pluck as a roll of the bar, the drums as six lanes of sixteen steps. The other voices become thin strips with their meters.
  • A voice that is muted or that rests is dim. A muted voice is red.

Words on these pages:

  • An envelope (env) shapes how a sound starts and fades each time it plays.
  • An LFO is a slow wobble that moves the Target up and down. S&H jumps to a random value each cycle. Each LFO can also move another voice or an effect. The piece stores this setting. Acid cut and Acid reso give the acid its own wah or wobble. Echo time wobbles the tape echo (at full depth, it sounds like a dub siren). The Punch cut target of the pad LFO sweeps the punch-in FILTER while you hold it (an auto-wah on the tracks that it takes). The Reso target of the bass LFO moves the resonance of the bass. The rates from 1/8 on lock the LFO to the beat.
  • Cutoff is the point where the filter starts to cut the high sounds (lower is darker). Reso (resonance) adds a sharp, strong peak at that point. Env mod is how far the envelope of the acid opens the filter on each note.
  • An accent is a louder note. A slide or glide bends smoothly into the next note.
  • Swing plays every second sixteenth note a little late. 50 % is straight (no swing).
  • A ratchet is a step that plays as a quick roll. A flam is two hits very close together.
  • Ghosts are very quiet hits between the main hits.
  • Send is how much of a track goes to the echo and reverb. A cent is a hundredth of a semitone.

On the Perform screen and the visualizers, the four knobs are the FX knobs: Filter, Crush, Throw and Wash (section 9).

What moves by itself

The chord changes on a bar line every few bars. The genre sets how often:

Bars per chordGenres

Drift on the Tempo page changes that rate. Below 20, the chords change four times as often. Below 45, they change twice as often. Above 80, they last twice as long. Above 95, they last four times as long. The Tempo page shows the result in bars.

In HYPNO, a chord lasts for a time, not for a number of bars. Drift sets 20 seconds to 5 minutes, with a small random change. The chord change then waits for the next bar line. In any genre, EDIT goes to the next chord.

Any piece can use bars or seconds. To change it, hold SEL and turn Drift right for bars or left for seconds. HYPNO in bars starts from four bars for each chord. The other genres in seconds work as HYPNO does. The piece stores this setting. The genres keep their own setting if you do not change it.

On a seven-note scale, each genre has its own habits for the next chord:

  • Lofi and DnB often move up a fourth (the ii-V-I turnaround of jazz).
  • House loops by steps and thirds.
  • Dubstep and trap stay near the home chord, on the dark side.
  • Dub stays on a minor vamp (a repeated pattern) between the first and fourth chords.
  • HYPNO keeps its original walk.

Lofi, DnB, breaks, garage, disco, reggae and amapiano use cadences (a strong pull back home). In a minor key, their V chord (built on the fifth note of the scale) raises the seventh note of the scale by a semitone, as in the harmonic minor. That note is the third of the V chord, and the acid and bass also raise it. Diminished chords (the tense chord on the seventh note of a major key, the second of a minor key) are rare in every genre. When a genre would lead home through a diminished chord, it goes through V instead.

The Loop setting on the Chords page repeats the last 2, 4 or 8 chords. It counts back from the chord that plays now. Thus, a loop of two is the home chord and one other chord. When you choose a new piece, the loop becomes empty. Most genres have a loop at the start: four chords in house, disco, garage, trap and synthwave, two in dubstep, DnB, electro, amapiano and trip-hop. Lofi (for its turnarounds), dub, funk, reggae and breaks keep walking.

The voices follow the chords. The pad's notes swell and fade on cycles of different lengths. Each note takes the pitch of the new chord when it swells back in. The bass follows the root of the chord and glides. The notes of the acid line are tied to the chord, so the riff moves with the chord. Mutate rewrites some of its steps each time the line repeats.

The pluck plays an arpeggio: the notes of the chord one after another. Motion sets how far the music slowly changes between sparse and full, over a minute or two. Evolve makes the acid and the chords change more.

Tempo. The tempo goes from 70 to 190. To set it from any page, hold HOME and turn SELECT. To tap the tempo, hold GLO and tap E4 approximately four times. When an outside MIDI clock controls PERF-1, PERF-1 follows that clock and the screen shows EXT.

Octaves

OCT- and OCT+ move one octave at a time, from three down to three up.

  • On the Pad, Bass or Acid pages (or their env and LFO pages), they move only that voice.
  • On the Pluck pages, they move the pluck, from two down to two up.
  • On all other pages, Tempo included, they move the whole song: pad, bass and acid together.

The line above the knobs shows the octave when it is not at home: OCT+2 for the voice of the page, SONG-1 for the song. The piece stores the octaves. When a voice goes down, it stops at the lowest note that you can hear (about C1). Thus, -2 and -3 can sound the same.

Filter types. The saw sounds of the pad (Strings, Dub, Trance, Poly, Rave, Brass, Fat, Pluck: on the other pad sounds, the page shows NO FILTER), the bass and the acid each go through a resonant filter. The standard type is low-pass (it cuts the high sounds). The FILTERS settings page (hold HOME: it is the first one) changes the type of each filter:

Making it scream. The SCREAM settings page (the next page after FILTERS) pushes the filters of the acid and the bass into a raw, screaming sound, like an acid line through a distortion pedal. It is part of the piece, like FILTERS: store the piece to keep it. At 0, everything sounds as before.

On an acid piece, start with Scream at 60 and Drive at 40. Then turn the Cutoff and Env mod of the acid (or let an LFO on Acid cut do it). If the acid gets too loud, turn its level down a little. The limiter holds it, but the limiter then makes the rest of the mix quieter.

The wub. In DUBSTEP, and with the Wub sound in any genre, the bass LFO on the cutoff makes a wub in the same way as a producer makes one. A clean sine sub stays below it. The oscillator of the sound goes through a resonant low-pass filter. The LFO sweeps this filter from about 70 Hz up to 3-4 kHz. A vowel band above it gives the "yoi".

After the filter, the sound is driven hard, so it growls more as the filter opens. It is also high-passed, so the sub stays clean. On the drop, the speed of the LFO follows a rhythm, beat by beat over two bars (1/4, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16, 1/2). Each piece has its own rhythm, and a second one every 8 bars (the Wub sound does this in any genre). DNB pieces 2 and 6 and INDUS pieces 4 and 8 have it. Through a build, the LFO speeds up from 1/4 to 1/16.

Depth sets how far the filter opens. Shape sets how it moves (Sine: closed on the beat, open between; Saw: open, then it closes; Square: open and shut; S&H: a level each cycle). Cutoff sets where it starts. Reso sets how much it sings. SCREAM on the bass drives it harder. For the old wobble, set the Target to any value other than Cutoff.

A wobble (the bass LFO moves the cutoff) works with every filter type. The filter types are part of the piece, and the piece stores them. This is the MultiFilter idea from Caustic, made again here.

Voicing sets how the chord is spread. Close keeps the four notes together. Open moves the second-highest note down an octave, which spreads the chord out. Comp sets how the pad plays the chords. Auto uses the rhythm of the genre for the stab sounds (short chords). Hold never plays stabs (a stab sound plays as a held pad, like a drone). The other seven settings force stabs on any pad sound (section 8, Feel, describes them).

Since 0.17.82, the Chorus of the pad (it makes the pad sound wider) is on the CHORUS settings page. The Root of the song is on Global.

  1. Set Chord to Dub.
  2. Choose the Dub sound for the pad (dark chord stabs).
  3. Turn the echo up.

Your own progression

On the Progression page, you write the chords yourself. To open it, hold EDIT or press ENV five times. A short push of EDIT still goes to the next chord.

The page has eight slots. Each slot holds one chord of the scale, its type and the number of bars that it lasts. The first eight white keys select the slot that you edit. Until you change a value, the page shows the chords that you heard last. You can start from those chords.

Pedal: follow the chords, or hold the key. White key 9 changes the bass between two modes, and white key 10 changes the pad:

  • Follows (the usual mode): the voice moves with each chord.
  • Pedal: the voice stays on the key while the chords move above it. The bass holds the root of the key. (A walking bass walks the chord of the key.) The pad holds the chord of the key (Am7 in A minor) while the progression goes round. This is a pedal point, the oldest drone method (a drone is one note that you hold for a long time). When the chords move away from the held note, the music gets tension. When the chords go back to I, the tension goes away. Use it when you play a guitar or a synth over the music.

While a voice is on Pedal, its key is lit, and the bottom of the page shows which voice it is. This is your setting, not a setting of the piece. It stays as you set it, when you change pieces and after power-off. The THEORY screen continues to show the chords as they move.

The chords are steps of the scale, not fixed notes. Thus they follow the key. If you change the root or the mode, the progression moves with it. The chord kind, the voicing (the order and spacing of the chord notes) and the other settings of the Chords page still apply. In the genres with a cadence, the V chord in a minor key gets its raised seventh, as it does in the walk.

While Play is on Loop, the walk, Drift and the chord Loop setting have no effect. To give the chords back to the genre, set Play to Walk. The genre then selects the chords again, and your slots stay for later. The progression is stored with the piece.

6Pieces, genres and storing

PERF-1 has twenty-two genres. Each genre is a bank of ten pieces. A piece is a ready-made song that you can play and change.

  • To select another piece in the bank, turn PRESETS. The number goes from 01 to 10, and then it continues from the other end.
  • To select another bank, hold GLO and turn PRESETS (this also continues from the other end). Or, use the Genre knob on the Global page.

When you change genre, the same piece number continues to play in the new genre. The effects of the new genre also come on. For example, lofi latches vinyl and tape on, and house latches the pump on (a latched effect stays on). The pump makes the music quieter on each kick, so the music seems to breathe with the beat. When you change genre, PERF-1 first clears all vinyl, tape, pump or phaser that you latched.

Each piece has its own settings, its own six drum patterns and its own drum sounds. Patterns 1 to 6 are approximately: intro, groove, build, peak, variation, and a thinner pattern to use in a mix. When you select a piece, the screen shows a made-up title, for example VELVET RAIN. The title is the same each time.

Storing a piece

You can store a piece in every genre. Go to Global and press SAVE. PERF-1 stores the piece as it is now: the values of every page, the scale and mode, the sounds and kit of the voices, the patterns and the drum sounds. PERF-1 stores it in the current genre, and it replaces the factory piece of that genre. For example, if you store piece 3 of lofi, piece 3 of lofi is yours from then on. Each genre has its own place in memory.

While the piece has changes, the line above the knobs shows 01 SAVE: STORE. After you store it, the line shows 01 STORED.

If you do not store your changes, they stay while you stay in that genre, also when you move between its pieces. When you change genre or switch off, these changes are lost. When you go back to the genre, its pieces are as they were before.

Back to factory. On Global, hold SEL and SAVE together for 1.5 seconds. While you hold, the screen shows KEEP HOLDING: FACTORY. The piece goes back to its factory settings, and its slot goes back to the factory piece. SAVE alone only stores.

Everything back to factory. Use this if you do not know what you stored by accident. Stop the music. Then, on Global, hold SEL and SAVE for 6 seconds. When you hold past the reset of the piece, the screen shows KEEP HOLDING: EVERY PIECE, ALL GENRES. Then PERF-1 goes through the genres one by one, and the screen shows how many pieces it put back.

Your settings, voices and set list do not change. If the music plays, PERF-1 resets only the piece, and the screen shows STOP FIRST.

Start from nothing. On Global, hold SEL and press OCT-. The screen shows EMPTY PIECE. The piece that plays keeps its sounds, kit, key and tempo. It loses all other settings: there are no drums, and the pad holds the home chord of the key. The bass, the acid and the pluck each change to its steps, and every step is a rest (no note). Nothing changes automatically (Evolve, drum drift, the answers and ghost notes are off). Then build the piece up:

  • Put the drums in the sequencer (hold SEQ, section 8).
  • Put the bass on Bass steps (LFO x5), the acid on Acid steps (ARP x5) and the pluck on Pluck steps (ARP x9). Select a step with a white key. Then turn its kind from rest to a note.
  • Put the chords on the Progression page (hold EDIT). Start with one chord, then add more slots.

SAVE stores it in the place of that piece. If you change your mind, hold SEL + SAVE to get the factory piece back.

When it is written. PERF-1 writes immediately. The FM-1 cannot make sound while it writes to its memory. Thus, if the music plays, the music fades out (the screen shows STORING). Then PERF-1 writes, and the music fades back in at exactly the same point. The pause is less than a second, and the song stays in time with itself. MIDI clock out (the tempo signal that PERF-1 sends to other devices) also pauses. Back to factory operates in the same way, and so does all other data that PERF-1 saves (section 13).

7Sounds, kits and playing

Each voice has its own list of sounds. Each drum can take its sound from any of twenty-three kits, and from three more kits that you load yourself. The sounds that you select are stored with the piece.

To change the sound of a voice, turn the Sound knob on its page. Or, hold SEL and turn ALGORITHM on any of its pages. On the pluck pages, SEL + ALGORITHM goes to the next sound that is really there. After the last sound, it goes back to the first. The voice keeps its notes, envelope (how the sound starts and fades), LFO (a slow wave that moves a setting) and filter. Only the sound changes.

Some words that this section uses: FM is the method that the DX7 and the FM-1 use to make sound. One tone bends another tone. FM is good for bells, electric pianos and metallic sounds. A saw is the waveform with a bright, buzzing sound that most analog synth sounds use.

Detuned means some copies of a sound, each slightly out of tune with the others. This makes the sound wider. A sub is an extra tone an octave below. A filter cuts the high sounds. When you open the filter, the sound becomes brighter.

Bass steps: write the bass line yourself

Use the Bass steps page to write your own bass line. To open it, press LFO five times (the page after Bass line). The page gives the bass sixteen steps (one bar), like the Acid steps and Pluck steps pages. When you open it, it already holds the bar that the bass played last. So you can start from a line that works and change what you want.

  • Press a white key to select a step. The sixteen white keys are the sixteen steps. The step that you selected is lit.
  • Step (KNOB1): sets rest, note, accent (louder), glide, accent and glide, or hold. With glide, the note slides in from the note before, without a new attack. With hold, the note before continues to sound through this step. Some holds in a row make a long note.
  • Note (KNOB2): the root, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th, 6th, 7th or octave, counted in the scale above the root of the chord. The notes follow the chords: a 5th is always the fifth of the chord that plays.
  • Octave (KNOB3): down, mid or up.
  • Mode (KNOB4): the mode of the bass line. Steps plays your steps. Any edit changes the mode to Steps. To try the other modes, turn the knob again. Your steps stay.

The steps are part of the piece. To keep them, save the piece (GLO, then SAVE). A shared piece also contains them. A piece without steps plays its own bass line.

The pluck: 12 + 60 + up to 16 sounds

The pluck is the counter-melody: a second, lighter line over the other parts. It plays an arpeggio: the notes of the chord one after another, not all at the same time. Two plucked voices take turns, so the end of each note continues to ring under the next note. The pluck plays in time with the bar, with swing on the off-sixteenths. Swing makes every second note play a little late, for a shuffle feel. In a breakdown, the pluck plays fewer notes.

By default, the pluck stays in the background, below the other parts (Level 50, Density 45). To turn it off, set Level to 0. Its two pages are Pluck and Pluck 2, on ARP (press it six times and seven times). The mood (section 10) changes its level, decay and density.

Pluck steps: freeze the pluck's loop and edit it

Use the Pluck steps page to keep a pluck phrase that you like, or to write your own. To open it, press ARP nine times (the page after Deja vu). The page holds the loop of the pluck and lets you edit it one step at a time.

The page has the same layout as Acid steps. It shows one bar as sixteen steps, and each step shows as a bar. The height is the note of the step. A note here is one of the notes of the chord, counted from low to high across the octaves of the pluck (1 is the lowest). The notes come from the chord that plays, so your line follows the harmony when the chord changes. An accent shows in the bright colour, a soft note shows dim, and a rest shows as a dash. A mark shows under the step that is playing.

  1. Press a white key to select a step.
  2. Turn Step to set what it plays: Rest, Soft, Note or Accent.
  3. Turn Note (1-12) to set its note.
  4. Turn Octave to move the whole pluck up or down (-2 to +2), the same as on the Pluck page. Range, on Pluck 2, sets how many octaves the notes cover.

Frozen holds the line. Any edit freezes the line. When the line freezes, PERF-1 also sets the Deja vu Loop to 16, the pluck Rate to 1/16 and Pluck vu to 100. So every note comes back the same in each bar. A frozen line stays when you start a new piece, and it is stored with the piece (a note and a kind for each step). If you set Frozen back to Free, the loop belongs to Deja vu again, and a new piece forgets it.

When the voice of the Jam page is on the pluck, Capture (SEL + REC) writes the bar that you just played into this page (section 10).

Acid steps: freeze the line and edit it

The acid line makes itself up as it plays. Mutate changes a small part of it each time through the line. To keep a line that you like and shape it, use the Acid steps page. To open it, press ARP five times (the page after Acid answer).

The page shows the line as sixteen bars, one for each step, the same way as Pluck steps. The height is the note. A short tail shows that the step slides into the next step. Press a white key to select a step. Then set the step with the knobs:

KnobDoes
StepRest, Note, Accent (louder and brighter), Slide (glides into the next note, as on a 303) or Acc+Slide
NoteThe note, 0 to 11 scale steps above the chord. The line follows the chord, so it moves when the chord changes.
Octave0 to 3 octaves up, added to the Acid octave
FrozenFree: the line continues to change (Mutate), and a new line grows with each piece. Frozen: the line stays as it is, and Mutate does not change it. The line is stored with the piece (Global, SAVE) and comes back with it.

Any edit freezes the line, so Mutate cannot change it. To let the line change again, turn Frozen back to Free. To keep the line that you hear now, turn Frozen to Frozen. The Length of the line, the answer (above) and the Acid octave all work as usual on a frozen line. Steps after the Length show dim and do not play. A piece stored with older firmware starts with a free line.

Deja vu: loops in the pluck, and what the acid mutates

Without Deja vu, the pluck selects every note new. The Deja vu page makes the pluck remember, so phrases come back. To open it, press ARP eight times.

KnobDoes
Pluck vuEach time a note comes back, the chance that it is the same note, rest or leap as the last time. 0: every note is new. 100: the loop is locked and repeats exactly.
LoopHow many notes it remembers: 4, 8, 16 or 32
LeapsHow often a new note jumps an octave
MotifHow often a bar develops the opening idea of the phrase (0 off, 100 always; 60 by default)

A locked phrase always plays over the current chord, so it follows the harmony. A change to the Loop length, or a new piece, makes the pluck forget the loop.

Motif makes the pluck play in phrases. The first bar of every eight bars is kept as an idea. The idea is which sixteenths played, and how far each note moved up or down the chord from the note before. Each bar after it then develops the idea, as often as the knob sets. The bar plays the same shape, starting from the current position of the line (so it follows the chords). Sometimes it plays the shape upside down, or with a new last step.

The fifth bar plays the idea again at its first pitch, as an answer to the first bar. Most phrases start a new idea. Some start with the old idea again. At 0, the pluck makes every bar new. Deja vu has priority over Motif: if Deja vu replays a note, the note plays again, whatever the motif says.

The acid has its own version of this: Mutate on the Acid line page. Mutate changes a few steps each time through the line. To select what a change does, hold SEL and turn the Mutate knob on that page:

  • All: anything.
  • Notes: the rhythm stays and the notes change slowly. This is the classic acid change.
  • Rhythm: the same notes come and go.
  • Accents: only the accents and slides.

Playing a voice by hand

The keys always play a voice over the music. On the pages of a voice, the keys play that voice, together with its own part. You can also take a voice away from the algorithm. Then only you play that voice:

  1. Go to one of the pages of the voice: ENV for the pad, LFO for the bass, ARP for the acid.
  2. Hold SEL and press ENV. The voice becomes silent and the line above the knobs shows BY HAND. (If the page is not a page of a voice, SEL + ENV only tells you this.)
  3. Play the keys. While the pages of that voice are on the screen (any page of the pad, the bass or the acid), the keys play the voice over the rest of the song.

How each voice plays:

  • Pad: Up to four notes at the same time, each on one of its voices. Attack and Release come from the Pad env page (they are quicker than the slow swells of the pad).
  • Bass: The last key that you hold, two octaves down. The envelope (the shape of the sound over time) opens on each new press.
  • Acid: The last key that you hold, an octave down, with its filter and envelope. If you hold a key and press a second key, the note slides to the second key, as on a 303.

All the other parts continue by themselves: the other voices, the chords and the drums. The keys follow the octave of the voice and the octave of the song (OCT- / OCT+). The lit keys still show the scale, so you can see which notes are in key. This also works when the music is stopped. A key plays until you release it, also if you change the page. On all other pages, the keys play the voice of the Jam page. On the Global page, the keys edit the scale.

To give the voice back to the algorithm, press SEL + ENV again on one of its pages. The piece stores which voices you play by hand. MIDI out sends the notes that you play, on the channel of the voice. To play the pluck, use the Jam page (section 10).

Links: the drums into the voices

Links let the drum hits change the voices, as trigger cables do on a modular synth. SEL + SEQ turns the links on and off. The links keep their settings. While the links are on, +LINK shows on the line above the knobs. Set each link on the Links page (press SEQ twice). 0 means off. The piece stores the links.

LinkWhat it does

8The sequencer

Use the sequencer to write the drum patterns. Hold SEQ to open it. Tap SEQ to go back to the Drums page.

  • There are six lanes, one for each drum: kick, snare, clap, closed hat, open hat and perc. Each lane has sixteen steps and its own colour.
  • The sixteen white keys are the steps of the drum that you are on. They come on where the drum hits. A moving light shows the position of the beat. Tap a key to turn that hit on or off.
  • OCT- and OCT+ select the previous or the next drum.
  • The knobs set these values for the drum: Level, Tune (+-1 octave), Decay, and Pattern (the pattern that you edit). Level goes from 0 to 150. Above 100, the drum is louder than the piece made it, up to +7 dB. Use this for a snare or hats that are too quiet. You can edit a pattern before it plays. A pattern change waits for the next bar.

While you hold SEL:

Black keys 1-6Copy this pattern into pattern 1-6
OCT-Clear the lane of this drum
OCT+Put back the piece's own lane

Tricks: SEL + a knob, on the lane that you are on:

SEL +Does

Steps: ratchets, push and level

Each lane also has step tricks: a ratchet, a push and a level for each of its sixteen steps. The step tricks are on a layer below the six patterns. Thus they apply to the pattern that plays, whichever pattern it is. Set them on the Steps page (press SEQ six times, or turn the dial past Groove).

  1. Turn KNOB 1 to choose the lane.
  2. Press a white key to choose the step. The key comes on, and the page shows the lane while it plays.
  3. Set the step with the other knobs:
KnobDoes
RatchetRepeats the hit inside its step, as a quick roll: x2, x3, x4, x6. A flam adds a quiet grace note (a short extra note) just before the hit. A swell plays three or four hits that get louder.

Some genres have tricks that are already set. In lofi, house, garage, hip-hop and most of the other genres, the hats between the eighth notes (the second and the fourth 16th of each beat) are soft. In house, disco and electro, the hat on the "and" of each beat (half a beat after the beat) has an accent. In DnB, breaks, funk and synthwave, the backbeat has an accent. In lofi and trip-hop, the snare is a little late (laid back). In garage and hip-hop, the snare is a very little late. In dub and reggae, the rim is a very little late. Trap has triplet rolls and a swell on its last hat beat.

9Effects

Use the effects to change the music live while it plays. Press FX to open the effects menu. Press it again to close it. While the menu is open, each key is an effect.

The keys have four banks of effects: FX, BEAT FX, FLEX BEAT and DJ SNIPS. Turn the SELECT dial to go from one bank to another. The dial stops at the first bank and at the last bank. The knobs become the FX knobs (see below).

The gesture recorder: loop your knob moves

Record up to four bars of FX knob moves and effects. Then they play again by themselves, and your hands are free for the keys.

  1. While the music plays, open the FX menu and press SEL + REC. The header shows ARM: the recorder waits for you.
  2. Turn an FX knob or play an effect key. This starts the recording (REC), from the start of the bar that plays now. For example, move the filter, crush the last beat, send to the echo, or hold a roll or a stutter. The recorder writes the knobs and the effect that you hold, every sixteenth.
  3. Press SEL + REC again. The recorder finishes the bar that plays now. Then it plays your moves in a loop (1 to 4 bars; after four bars, it starts the loop by itself). The header shows LOOP. An effect that you play by hand has priority. The effect of the loop comes back when you release the key.

If you turn a knob yourself, you control that knob again. The other knobs continue to play the loop. While the loop plays, press SEL + REC in the FX menu to release all the knobs. The knobs stay where they are. OCT- in the FX menu (it puts everything back to home) also releases them. PERF-1 does not save the loop, the same as the knobs. The horns, the OH!s, the crash, the kick and the snare are samples (recorded sounds). The air horn, the kick and the OH! are recordings in the public domain (CC0, from freesound.org: see LICENSING.md). PERF-1's own models made the crash. The horns play on the root note of the chord that plays now. Tap AIR HORN for one short blast, tap it quickly for a run of blasts, and hold it for the long note. The OH!s play as recorded. Up to four snips can sound at the same time. A snip plays when you press its key. Snips do not latch.

Land (the SNIP LAND page, after the SNIPS page) sets the timing of the RISER, REWIND and SNR ROLL. Set it to Beat, 2 beats or Bar. Then these three snips time themselves to the next line of that type that is at least that far away. They start immediately, take exactly the time to get to that line, and reach their peak on it. The rewind pulls back into the downbeat, and the riser gets to its top on the line. The roll (on the grid: eighths, then sixteenths, then thirty-seconds) ends with the drop.

If Land is on Bar and you press one of these snips just after a bar line, it takes nearly two bars, because the next line is too near. They play to the line even if you release the key. Now (the default) plays them as before, with a length in seconds. The other snips always play immediately when you press the key.

The snips that have a pitch play in key. The horns, the stab and the riser start on the root note of the chord that plays now (the stab plays that chord). The sirens, beeps, whoops, the dub siren and the bell use the nearest note of that chord. All the snips have about the same loudness. The horns, sirens, stabs, crash, riser and roll are wide in stereo (in mono, nothing is lost).

The Snips settings page has four settings:

  • Pitch: normal, an octave up, or an octave down.
  • Play: HELD or ONE SHOT. In HELD, the long sounds (the horns, sirens, dub siren, riser and snare roll) continue for as long as you hold the key, and the other sounds play to their end. In ONE SHOT, every snip plays to its end, and the long sounds last about a second.
  • Echo: dry, some or lots. This sets how much of the snips goes to the echo and the reverb.
  • Level: how loud the snips are.

While the Snips page shows, the keys play the snips. Use this to try the snips.

What some of these settings mean:

  • Mix is the balance between the plain sound and the effect. Reso (resonance) makes the filter ring and whistle at its setting. Feedback sends the effect back into itself, for a stronger result. Targets selects the parts of the music that the effect changes.
  • Gate patterns. Euclid 5, 7 or 9 puts 5, 7 or 9 hits over the steps, with spaces that are as equal as possible. Custom is a pattern that you edit yourself (below).
  • Roll. From and To set the speed of the repeats at the start and at the end. Each sets how often a roll occurs.
  • Riser. Time sets how long the riser takes to climb. Filter, Wash and Echo set how much of each the riser builds up.
  • Settings page. Key is the key change at the drop (section 13). The second knob has no function.
  • SCREEN page. Zoom shows the name and value of a knob in large text when you turn it. Saver is the screensaver. Lights makes the key lights follow the music. Look adds scanlines (every second row is a quarter darker, like an old CRT TV) and Calm (the small movements on the screen are off).
  • USB & MIDI page. Level sets if the USB audio follows the MASTER knob or stays at a fixed level. With a fixed level, a recording does not change when you turn the volume. MIDI out sends notes out through USB. MIDI map sets where the notes go (section 14).
  • Repeat page. Pitch makes each repeat move toward that pitch over four beats while you hold the key, and this sounds like a build. Each repeat still starts on the beat grid. Decay makes each repeat quieter than the one before it, for a tail in dub style. Gate sets how much of each slice sounds: all of it, 3/4, half or 1/4. Refresh takes a new slice of the newest music every beat or bar (DJs call this a slip roll). Refresh works only when enough recent music is held in memory.

The Gate page. The white keys show the steps of the pattern. A key is lit where the gate opens, and a mark shows the step that plays now. Press a key to edit a step, and the pattern becomes "custom". A pattern of 12 or 10 steps against the bar of 16 moves away from the beat and then comes back to it. This is called a polymeter. Gate 16 and Gate 8 share this page.

10Performing

These are the actions to play a set live. Most of them wait for the next bar, so all the music stays in time.

  • Breakdown: tap SAVE. In the bar before the breakdown, the percussion, the open hi-hat and half of the hats stop, so the energy goes down. On the first beat of the next bar, the groove stops with a last kick, a crash and the root note of the bass. PERF-1 sends the echo hard at that moment, so the bar before continues to sound into the breakdown. Then the drums stop.

In house, techno and dub, the kick continues. In drum and bass and industrial, the hats continue. In breaks and funk, the breakdown has only the drums. The mix becomes darker, the bass filter closes and the pad becomes louder. The breakdown has no fixed length: it continues until you do the next action.

  • Build: tap SAVE again. A snare roll goes up in pitch while the bass filter opens. The build lasts four bars. In lofi, trip-hop, hip-hop and ambient, it lasts two bars. In house, dubstep, DnB, psytrance and trance, it lasts eight bars. In the last bar, the open hat plays on every eighth note. In the four-on-the-floor genres (a kick on every beat), the kick comes back, and then the drop comes.
  • Drop: hold SAVE. All the parts come back on the next bar. SAVE comes on during a breakdown or a build. It blinks while it waits for the bar.
  • Freeze: REC. The chord stays and the reverb continues to sound. Press REC again to make the music move on.
  • Next chord: EDIT. Goes to the next chord at the next bar. If your own progression plays, it goes to the next slot.
  • The scale: the keys on Global. On the Global page, each key adds its note to the scale or removes it, on a keyboard picture of the scale. The root note always stays in the scale. On all other pages, the keys play a voice, so a key that you press by mistake never changes the scale. On Global and on the Chords page, ALGORITHM selects a mode (a ready-made scale). On all other pages, ALGORITHM sets the mood.
  • Patterns. To change the drum pattern, turn KNOB4 on the Drums page, or press SEL + white keys 1-6. A change waits for the next bar. If you turn the knob past several patterns, only the pattern where you stop plays.

Drum drift makes the drums change automatically while you play. Press SEQ four times to go to it. It is off by default. It has four settings:

  • Change goes to the next pattern, or to a random pattern from pattern 1 up to the Pool number.
  • Every sets how often a change occurs: after a set number of bars, or with each chord change. The bar before each change ends in a fill: the snare and hats play a roll into the change.
  • Pool sets the highest pattern for a random change. A random change selects a pattern from pattern 1 up to this number.
  • Mutate changes the pattern a little at the same moments. Shift moves one or two hits by one step. Morph also adds or removes a hit.

The kick and the clap always stay as written. A snare on a beat never moves. This keeps the groove steady.

The changes that Mutate makes stay in the pattern. To keep them, store the piece. Mutate also works when Change is off: then it slowly changes one pattern into a new pattern. Your Drum drift settings stay the same when you change the piece or the genre.

PERF-1 does not save songs. To keep a performance, record the audio (or the MIDI out) with the recording software on your computer. Keep the recordings that you like.

The auto-DJ

The auto-DJ plays the breakdowns, builds and drops for you. Then you can give your attention to the effects and the mix. Press GLO + F5 to start it. DJ shows in the header. The Perform screen counts the bars through each part. The auto-DJ goes through these sections, always on the bar:

  1. A groove of 8, 16, 32 or 64 bars (16 by default).
  2. A drum fill in the last bar of the groove, if Fill is on.
  3. A breakdown: short (4 bars), half the length of the groove (at least 4 bars, the default), or full (as long as the groove).
  4. A build (its length depends on the genre). The riser (a sound that goes up) climbs. The music stops for the last two sixteenth notes. Then the drop comes: all the parts come back together on the first beat. The drop has the kick, a clap and an open hat that rings like a crash. Usually the drop is on the home chord of the key.
  5. The groove again.

Set Groove, Break, Fill and Scenes on the Auto DJ settings page. Groove has a fifth setting, Sync. With Sync, the groove continues until the chord changes. (Drift on the Tempo page sets how long a chord lasts.) The bar before a chord change is a fill. The bar of the change is the breakdown.

Turn on DJ new pieces (GLO + G5). Then the auto-DJ brings in the next piece in the bank on the bar of the fill. Thus the breakdown and the drop are part of the new piece. If you press SAVE yourself, the auto-DJ continues from that point. If the key change on the drop is on (section 13), the key changes on each drop.

Auto FX

Auto FX plays effects automatically. It works as a DJ who adds a filter sweep or a tape stop before the next phrase. Turn it on from the Auto FX settings page. To get there, hold HOME, then turn SELECT. The page comes directly after Auto DJ. Auto FX works with or without the auto-DJ.

  • Each effect ends on a phrase line (the start of the next phrase). When Every is at 8 bars, an effect leads into bar 9, 17, 25 and so on. The effect ends exactly when the next phrase starts. Length is the longest time that an effect can take: a beat, half a bar, a bar or two bars. Short effects, such as a throw or a tape stop, always stay short.
  • It selects the effects that suit the moment. In a groove, it uses gates, repeats, sweeps and filters. In a breakdown, it uses washes such as the reverse swell, freeze and tape. In a build, it uses rolls and rising filters. At the end of a build (Medium and Wild), a stop, reverse, brake or spin-back leads into the drop. Auto FX plays no effect in the four bars after a drop.
  • It knows the genre. When Pick is on Genre, the effects of each genre come four times as often. These are throws and tape in dub and reggae, the pump and filters in house and disco, scratches and repeats in breaks and funk, hat rolls and half time in trap and dubstep, and gates and flangers in psy. Any gives all the effects the same chance. Yours plays your own gesture instead. It plays the gesture once, complete, and ends it on the phrase line. To record a gesture, use SEL + REC in the FX menu (see the gesture recorder).
  • Auto FX sets the amount. Gentle uses only washes and sweeps. Medium adds gates, filters, repeats and rolls. Wild adds stops, brakes, scatter and half time. Auto FX never plays the same effect twice in a row. Sometimes it skips a phrase line (more often at Gentle).
  • It does not interfere with you. No automatic effect plays while you hold or latch an effect yourself. When you play an effect, the automatic effect stops immediately. Auto FX also waits while the FX menu is open, during the intro, and when the music is stopped.

The header shows the name of each effect while it plays. The settings stay after power-off.

The SET settings page controls a longer set. Hold HOME to see the settings. The SET page comes after Auto FX on the dial.

  • Rounds gives each piece 1, 2, 4 or 8 rounds before DJ new pieces goes to the next piece. A round is one groove, breakdown, build and drop. The header and the Perform screen count the rounds, like R2/4.
  • Ramp makes the tempo increase during the rounds of groove of a piece, by +2, +4, +8 or +12 BPM. The tempo increases one BPM at a time. If you change the tempo manually, the increase continues from the new tempo. The next piece starts at its own tempo.
  • Intro builds up the music slowly from silence. When you press Play, the tracks come in one at a time over 1, 2, 4 or 8 minutes. The pad starts in the first bar. Then the hats, the perc, the kick, the snare, the bass, the pluck and last the acid come in. Each track comes in a seventh of the way further through the intro. This is useful when you mix in from another deck. It is also useful when you scratch or sample over only a pad and a hat. The header counts down, like INTRO 1:42. The auto-DJ stays in its groove until all the tracks are in. To start the intro again, stop the music and press Play again. Break is different. With Break, a piece that you start with PLAY or select with PRESETS starts in its breakdown. Thus you can go from piece to piece through a breakdown. Then you bring in each piece with SAVE (the build, then the drop). The new pieces that the auto-DJ brings in do not start in a breakdown.
  • Mix changes pieces as a DJ changes records. A piece can come in on the bar: the next piece from the auto-DJ, the next slot of the set list, or SEL + PRESETS. This piece starts at the tempo that was playing. Then its tempo changes slowly to its own tempo over 4, 8, 16 or 32 bars. Cut changes immediately to the new tempo, as before. Some set lists go from a slow piece to a fast piece. With these set lists, a long Mix moves the tempo of the whole set up (or down) smoothly. If you turn the tempo manually, the slow change stops at the tempo it has.

In the example above, the intro builds into slot 2. Slot 3 comes in as a breakdown. Slot 3 builds into the peak.

  • The auto-DJ brings in the next slot, not the next piece in the bank. (DJ new pieces does not have to be on.) It changes on the bar of the fill, after the Rounds of the piece. Thus the breakdown and the drop are part of the new piece. If a slot has Bars set, the slot changes when its bars are complete. The breakdowns and builds of the DJ still come in between.
  • The set list skips empty slots. After the last slot, it goes back to the first slot.

The list on the screen shows the genre and the piece of each slot. The slot that plays is bright. The slot that you edit has a bar behind it. The white keys light the slots that are in use. The key of the slot that plays blinks.

Track codes

Each time you press PLAY, PERF-1 starts a new track. It gives the track a code of nine letters, like DZBT-4DTT9. The screen shows the code when the music starts (PLAYING DZBT-4DTT9). The code holds the genre, the piece, the key, the mode, the tempo, and the dice (the random values) that each generator starts from. If you start the same code again, you hear the same track, note for note from the start. You hear the same chords, drums, bass line, acid and pluck. When you like a track, write down its code, or share the code in a comment.

To open the Code page, press GLO three times (Global, Set list, Code). The page shows the code that plays on nine tiles, and a strip of its bits. It also shows what the code starts: the genre and piece, the key and mode, and the tempo.

KnobSettingWhat it does
KNOB1PlaceThe letter that you change (the tile with the bar).
KNOB2LetterThe letter in that place. The last letter is a check letter, and it comes from the other letters. Thus one wrong letter shows as NOT A CODE.
KNOB3RollA new code with the same genre, piece, key and tempo, but other dice. Each turn gives a different code.

PLAY on the Code page starts the code that shows. This is the code that you typed or rolled, or the track that plays, again from the start.

What a code keeps and what it does not keep:

  • A code gives the piece as it comes (its recipe), in the key, mode and tempo of the code. If you changed the piece (a knob, a pattern, a stored version), the page shows EDITED: THE CODE GIVES THE RECIPE. The code still starts the track, but without your changes. To keep your changes, store the piece and share it through the library.
  • The same track plays while you let it play without changes. If you turn a knob or play a key, the track changes from that point. A scene, the FX or a change from the auto-DJ also change the track from that point.
  • If you start the music while the pad still rings, the first sound of the pad can be different. The notes are the same.
  • Codes are for this firmware. A later version can change how the generators work. Then an old code starts a different track.

The FM-1 keeps the set list together with the theme, Tune and MIDI out. The set list plays your stored pieces as you stored them, and the factory pieces as they come.

Mood

Mood is one control that makes the whole piece calmer or more intense. ALGORITHM sets it on all pages except Chords and Global. On those two pages, ALGORITHM selects the mode. 0 is chill, 100 is peak, and 50 (the middle, and the default) keeps the piece as it is.

The more you turn from 50, the more Mood changes these:

  • the density and the filter cutoff of the acid (how bright it is)
  • the levels of the hats and the perc
  • the brightness of the pad
  • the density, level and decay of the pluck (shorter toward peak)
  • the cutoff and the drive of the bass
  • the reverb and the echo (less toward peak)
  • Evolve
  • the level of the drums

Mood changes what you hear, but it does not change the stored piece. When you change the piece, Mood stays where you set it. The FM-1 does not keep Mood when you power off.

The keys' lights

On most pages, the key lights show the scale. While the music plays, the notes of the chord that plays are bright, and the other notes of the scale are dim. Thus you can see which keys fit at this moment. When the music is stopped, all the notes of the scale are lit.

Accents (SEL). The keys of the FM-1 cannot sense how hard you push them. To play an accent, hold SEL while you play a key. An accent is a louder note. On the acid, it is a full accent (brighter, with more punch). On the Jam page, SEL + a key always plays a note. It does not change the pattern as it does on other pages.

The phrase recorder (REC on the Jam page). Use it for a line that is longer than a bar, with long notes and rests. Record the line while you play it:

  1. Go to the Jam page. Make sure that the music plays and that the voice is the pluck, the acid or the bass. Press REC. The header shows ARM and REC blinks: the recorder waits for you. (On the Jam page, REC is the phrase recorder. On all other pages, REC is still Freeze.)
  2. Play. Your first key starts the recording (REC), from the start of the bar that you are in. The recorder writes each key on its sixteenth note, with the time that you held it. Hold SEL for an accent.
  3. Press REC to stop the recording at the end of the bar that you are in. (After four bars, it stops automatically.) If you press REC on the one (the first beat of the bar) or just after it, the recording ends with the bar before. Thus you can push REC on the downbeat, as on a looper. From then on, the phrase plays again and again, exactly as you played it. Long notes stay long, and rests stay rests. The line of the voice is off while the phrase plays. The header shows PHR.
  4. Press REC again on the Jam page (or SEL + REC on any other page) to release the phrase. The line of the voice comes back.

A phrase of one bar is also written into the line of the voice (as Capture does, below). Thus the phrase stays when you save the piece. Longer phrases play until you release them. They are not saved with the piece. The notes play as you played them. When the chords change under a long phrase, the phrase does not follow them.

On the pluck, a chord (the Chords mode of the Jam page) plays back as the full chord, with the same notes that you heard. The acid and the bass play one note at a time, so they record a chord as its root (the note that gives the chord its name). (The line of the pluck keeps one note for each step. Thus a one-bar phrase that goes into the line keeps the root of the chord. The phrase itself plays the chord.)

Capture (SEL + REC). The fastest way to write a riff (a short musical line) is to play it. Capture takes the bar that you played last on the Jam page. (Of the last four bars, it takes the bar where you played the most.) Capture makes that bar the line of that voice, as you played it. It sets each note against the chord that played under it. If you played a key a little early, Capture puts it on the step that you meant. The line starts where the bar starts:

  • With the voice on acid, the bar goes into the Acid steps page as a 16-step line, set to Frozen. Each sixteenth where you played a key gets that note. The notes are stored relative to the chord at that time, so the line follows the chords from then on. A key that you played on a beat becomes an accent (a louder note). A key that you hold sounds for as long as you held it. The steps that it covers get the same note, with a slide from the note before. Thus it plays as one long note (the tie of the 303). The line plays when you leave the Jam page.
  • With the voice on the pluck, the bar goes into the Deja vu loop, set to Loop 16, Rate 1/16 and Pluck vu 100. The loop starts at the next bar line. To let the line change again, turn Pluck vu down.
  • With the voice on the bass, the bar goes into the Bass steps page, and the Mode of the bass line changes to Steps. Each note becomes a scale step above the root of the chord, in the octave nearest to the note that you played. A key on a beat is an accent. A key that you hold continues to sound. The steps that it covers become Hold steps, so the note lasts as long as you held it. (If you still hold the key when you press SEL + REC, the note lasts to the end of the bar.)
  • You cannot capture the pad.

Capture remembers your last 32 key presses. First, play the bar on the Jam page. Then press SEL + REC (on the Jam page or on any other page). The header shows CAPTURED.

The master bus

The master bus is the last stage before the sound goes out of the FM-1. It makes the volume more even and stops clipping (distortion when the sound is too loud). After the effects, and before the MASTER volume, the mix goes through two stages:

  • a compressor. It turns the loud parts down, so the mix sounds tighter and more even. It works on the two sides of the stereo together.
  • a limiter: a hard ceiling that the sound cannot go above. It looks a moment ahead (it delays the sound by about 1.4 ms, which is too short to hear). Thus it is ready before a peak comes. It releases over about 60 ms.

After the MASTER volume, there is also a gentle soft clip as a last protection.

KnobRangeDoes
RatioOff, 1.5:1, 2:1, 3:1, 4:1, 6:1, 10:1How strongly the compressor reduces the sound. Higher is stronger. It starts gently (a 6 dB soft knee).
Thresh-30 to 0 dBThe threshold: how loud the sound must be before the compressor starts to work.
Make-up0 to +12 dBTurns the sound up again after the compressor.
Ceiling-12 to 0 dBThe ceiling of the limiter, before the MASTER volume.

The compressor does not listen to the deep lows (below about 100 Hz). Thus the kick and the sub do not pull the whole mix down on each beat. It reacts in 10 ms, so the hit of a drum gets through before the compressor turns it down. This keeps the punch.

The compressor releases in about 80 ms. It releases more slowly (up to half a second) when it turns the sound down a lot. On average, it gives back most of what it takes. Thus the level stays near where it was, and the knobs change the feel, not the volume.

The page shows the curve of the compressor. The sound that goes in is along the bottom, and the sound that comes out is up the side. The dotted line is where the input and the output are equal. The vertical tick is the threshold, and the horizontal tick is the ceiling. There are also live meters: COMP (how much the compressor turns the sound down), LIMIT (how much the limiter turns it down) and OUT (the peak of the output).

The piece keeps the four settings. The default is a light 1.5:1 at -12 dB, with +1.2 dB of make-up. Each genre has its own settings. Lofi and garage use 2:1. House, drum and bass and electro use 3:1. Breaks, dubstep and trap use 4:1 with more make-up. The ceiling of the limiter is 0 dB in every genre, so it only catches sounds that would clip.

The CHORUS page

This settings page comes after SOUND. It is a chorus in the style of the Juno-106 chorus, and it makes the pluck and the acid wide. As on the Juno, a slow triangle sweeps one short delay. It sweeps the other side in the opposite direction. The chorus mixes this with the dry sound. The lows (under about 200 Hz) stay dry in the middle, so the low end stays mono. These are the settings of the unit, the same for every piece.

KnobWhat it does

The rotary moves the sound from side to side with a small change of pitch, like the turning horn of an organ. When it changes from one speed to the other, it gets faster or slower over about a second, as the real one does. Try it with an organ DX voice on the pluck.

The RESONATOR page

This page comes after CHORUS. It has three ringing filters, tuned to the chord that plays (its root, third and fifth). The drums that you select strike them. A rim, a perc tick or a hat then sounds pitched, in the key. The ringing follows each chord change. These are the settings of the unit, the same for every piece.

KnobWhat it does
AmountHow loud the ringing is: 35 by default (0.17.54). 0 is off.
DecayHow long it rings. Low is short and woody (a marimba, a log drum). High is long and glassy (a bell, a steel tine). The level stays almost the same across the range.

TargetsWhat strikes the filters (Perc by default): the Perc, the Hats, the Snare (and clap), Perc+Hats, all the Drums except the kick, or the Pluck.

The ringing is about equally loud in all pieces. It follows how hard the sound strikes it, not the level of that track. The first note is in the middle, the second a little to the left, the third a little to the right, with some reverb. With the Pluck, it rings only when the pluck plays in the octave of the Register: try each Register. Try Perc on an Industrial piece, or Hats with a long Decay on an ambient piece. When the load is very high, it switches off automatically.

The RETURNS page

This page comes after RESONATOR. It sets how the kick ducks the returns of the echo and the reverb (the sound that comes back from them). Thus a repeat or a tail gets louder between the kicks, and does not smear over them. Only the returns change. The dry sound, the sound that goes into the echo and the reverb, and the repeats of the echo do not change. These are the settings of the unit, the same for every piece.

KnobWhat it does
DuckKit (the default): as each kit sets it, the same as before. Off: the returns stay steady. Custom: use the two knobs below.
DepthWith Custom: how far the returns go down on each kick, 60 by default. 100 is the deepest.
ReleaseWith Custom: how long the returns take to come back, in the tempo: 1/16 (fast) to 1 bar (they rise and fall with the bar). The kit's release is about 3/16.

The kick must play, or nothing ducks. The returns are a small part of the sound of most pieces. Thus the change is small until you turn up the echo or the reverb. Try a dub throw (THROW, or the DELAY punch-in on the snare) over a four-on-the-floor kick with Custom, Depth 100, Release 1/2.

While a visualizer shows, the knobs are still the FX knobs, ALGORITHM still sets the mood, and the keys still set the track mix, as on Perform. FX opens its menu over the picture. All drawings use the colours of your theme. They follow the kicks and snares, the level of each part, the chord, the pattern and the section.

The inner ring shows the key signature of each key (0, 1#, 2# .. 6#, 5b .. 1b). The signature of your key is lit, so you can read "E major has 4 sharps" directly from it. The scale is one arc on the circle (the seven notes of a mode are always neighbours on the circle of fifths). Each chord has its Roman numeral on the arc (upper case is major, lower case is minor, ° is diminished). The colour of each numeral shows its job, in the groups that theory books use: the tonic's family (I, iii, vi) has the pad's colour, the subdominant's family (ii, IV) has the bass's colour, and the dominant's family (V, vii) has the acid's colour. The name of the function shows on the right in the same colour. The chord that plays is filled, and the next chord has a ring. A curved arrow goes from one to the other. Thus you see the progression jump around the circle. The outer ring names every note the way the key spells it: G# in E major (not Ab), Cb in Eb minor, E# in F# major. The notes of the chord are marked. A note borrowed from outside the scale, like the G# in the E7 of A minor, is red. The right side: the numeral of the chord, its name and its notes (with the same spelling). Then what the chord does (tonic, subdominant, dominant ..; V OF THE NEXT in red when the genre makes it the dominant of the next chord, BORROWED when it comes from the parallel minor). Then the next chord and how the root moves to it (a fifth down, a step up; "home" when it falls a fifth to the tonic). Then the eight slots of your progression as a chord chart. At the bottom: the key and its signature, with the major key that it is written in ("0: C MAJOR" for A minor). When your progression is off, the slots show the genre's walk as you heard it.
AUTOSelects one of the light shows (not THEORY). It changes to a new one at each section change and every 32 bars.

Backing up and restoring

If you install another firmware, or install PERF-1 again, the data that PERF-1 stored can be erased. The Back up and restore card on the installer page (below the DX7 voices) keeps this data safe:

  • Save a backup reads all the data that the FM-1 stored and saves it as a .p1bak file: the pieces that you saved in every genre, the settings, the DX7 voices, the theme, Tune and MIDI out. Do this before you install anything else. The backup does not include changes to a piece that you did not store (store them first, section 6). It also does not include the panel calibration, because the calibration belongs to that one unit.
  • Restore the chosen backup (select the file first) writes all the items in the file back, one item at a time. Then the FM-1 restarts. The backup replaces the data that is stored now. The music fades out for each write, and the FM-1 saves nothing else until the restart. A backup from an older PERF-1 loads into a newer one. If the firmware does not recognise an item in the file, it refuses the item and does not write it.
  • Only PERF-1 reads these files. The FM-1 must run PERF-1 0.14 or later. An older version does not answer, and the page tells you this.

Sharing pieces

The Pieces page on the PERF-1 website shares one piece at a time. You can send a piece that you made to another person, and you can add the pieces that other people share. Connect the FM-1 with a USB cable and open the page in Chrome or Edge.

What goes with a piece: all of its settings, its six drum patterns and step tricks, a frozen acid or pluck line, your chord progression, and the DX7 voice that its pluck plays, if you loaded that voice yourself (section 15). Your effects settings, the theme and the set list do not go with the piece.

To share a piece:

  1. Store the piece on the FM-1 (GLO, then SAVE). You can share only stored pieces.
  2. On the Library & pieces page of the website, open My FM-1. Press Read my pieces and set list. Tick the piece (or tick several: Tick all ticks all of them).
  3. If you want, add a title, your name and a note (what to play, what to try). If you tick several pieces, your name goes with each one. Each piece keeps its own genre and number as its name.
  4. Submit to the Library puts the piece in the Library for everyone immediately. Download .piece saves a small file (several pieces download as one .zip). Copy link copies a link that opens the page with your piece in it, ready to add (for several pieces: one link on each line). Copy code copies the same data as text that starts with P1.. Use the code where links do not work.

To add a shared piece:

  1. Open the link, paste the code into the box, or select the .piece file. The page shows the genre and number of the piece, its tempo, key and pluck sound, and its title and note, if it has them.
  2. Select where it goes. It always goes into its own genre. You select which of the ten pieces it replaces. It starts on the number that it had.
  3. Press Add to my FM-1. If you already stored a piece there, the page asks you before it replaces it.
  4. The FM-1 restarts to load the piece. Then go to the genre (GLO + PRESETS) and the piece (PRESETS). Store the piece that you played before you add a piece. The restart loses the changes that you did not store.

Its DX7 voice. If the pluck of the piece plays its own DX7 voice, the voice comes with the piece:

  • If that voice is already loaded on your FM-1, nothing changes, and the piece uses your voice.
  • If the slot of the voice on your FM-1 is empty, the voice goes into that slot.
  • If the slot holds a different voice of yours, the page asks you to select: put the new voice in your next empty slot (none of your data changes), or replace your voice in that slot. If you replace it, the sound changes in all of your pieces that use the old voice.

Hear it in the emulator puts the piece into the browser emulator (section 17) instead, with its voice. Thus you can listen to it before you add it to your FM-1.

Sharing a whole set. The first row after Read my pieces and set list is My set list: your set list (section 10) with every piece in it, their DX7 voices, Play and the Mix of the SET page. Your stored pieces go with it. A factory piece is the same on every FM-1, so the page sends only its place. The set downloads as a .perfset file, or copies as a link or a code. The link or code is long: for a set with many stored pieces, share the file where links get cut short, for example in a Discord message.

When you add a set, each stored piece goes back to its own number in its genre. Its voices go into your empty voice slots (if you already have a voice, the FM-1 uses it as it is). The set also replaces your set list. Before it does this, the page shows the list of your stored pieces that it would replace, and asks you.

The card. Card draws a picture of the piece or set, to post with its link. The picture shows its name, title, maker, tempo and key, and its drum pattern as rings (one ring for each drum, the kick inside, sixteen steps around). For a set, it shows the places of the set as a ring. Save the card (PNG) downloads the picture.

Based on. When you add a piece from a share, this browser remembers where the piece came from. If you change it, store it and share it again, it goes out as "based on" the original (its title and maker), and the card shows this. If you share it without changes, the page fills in its original title and maker for you.

The Library. The Library tab of the Library & pieces page collects pieces and sets that people made. Each one has its card, title, maker and note. You can hear it in the emulator or add it to your FM-1 (Hear or add). You can filter the Library by genre, search it, or show only the ones that the maker picked, the newest ones, or yours.

To put your pieces in, tick them on My FM-1 and press Submit. They are there immediately, for everyone, with no account. The Library checks each submission first: it must be a real PERF-1 piece or set, and the title, name and note must be short and plain. You can remove a submission from the same browser that you submitted it from: its card in that browser has Remove.

Versions. You and the other person both need PERF-1 0.16.3 or later to add a piece. A piece made on an older PERF-1 loads into a newer one: settings that it does not have yet play as the piece's genre sets them. A piece made on a newer PERF-1 loads into an older one without the newer settings, and the page tells you this.

The Global page (GLO) has four knobs:

  • Root: the key of the music.
  • Theme: the colours: Console (the default), Imp, Synth, Acid, Ice, Ember or Phosphor. All themes use the same layout (section 4). Only the colours change.
  • Tune: fine tuning, +-50 cents (a cent is a hundredth of a semitone).
  • Genre: the bank of pieces. It is the same as GLO + PRESETS.

MIDI out and the USB audio level are on the USB & MIDI settings page. Hold HOME to show the settings. Then turn SELECT to the page.

In a theme, each voice always has the same colour on all pages. In Imp, the pad is bone white, the bass is crimson, the acid is lime, the pluck is ice-teal and the drums are pink. Phosphor uses only one green. The accent colour shows what is happening now. If a piece waits for the next bar, the screen shows >08 next to the key and mode.

Hold SEL alone. A card shows what shift does on this page. The SCREEN settings page has Look: plain, scanlines, calm (no small movements) or calm with scanlines.

The theme, Tune and MIDI out settings apply to all pieces.

Playing the chords from a keyboard (CHORDS). This mode works like an arranger keyboard. Your left hand plays the chords, and PERF-1 plays the band around them. Set Notes on the MIDI IN page to CHORDS. Then play a keyboard that sends MIDI to the FM-1 (on any channel, or on the channel that you choose):

  • Below middle C, the FM-1 reads the keys as a chord. The song follows the chord immediately: the pad plays it, the bass plays the lowest key, and the acid and the pluck change the scale to fit it. The screen shows the chord that it received (CHORD IN Dm7). When you release the keys, the chord stays until you play the next chord. The walk and the progression wait.
  • The FM-1 knows the usual chords: major, minor, 7, maj7, m7, dim, aug, sus4, sus2, m7b5, dim7, m(maj7), 7sus4, 6, m6, add9, m(add9), 9, maj9, m9, 7b5, 7#5, and the 7ths without their fifth. The lowest key is the bass: E G C is C over E (a first inversion). A key under a chord that does not contain that key makes a slash chord (D under C E G is C/D). One key plays the chord of the scale on that key (for a key outside the scale, it plays the major chord). This is the quickest way to change the chords.
  • From middle C up, the keys play the voice, the same as with KEYS. This is the melody on top.
  • If you set Notes to a different setting, the chord control stops. The walk continues from the last chord.

The link: two units as one band. Two units with PERF-1 (two FM-1s, or an FM-1 and a PERF-1 SMK) find each other and play together. One unit leads, and the other unit follows.

  1. Connect the two units. There are two ways: - The cable (simplest): use one plain USB-C to USB-C cable between the two units, with no hub and no computer. Set Link to Cable lead on one unit and to Cable follow on the other unit (on the LINK settings page, the last page). The port then carries only the link, not USB. Each unit uses its own power (battery or its own charger). The third line of the page counts the link messages (WIRE OUT, IN, BAD). The FM-1 never keeps the cable setting: after a power-off, the port is plain USB again. Thus the installer always finds the unit. - Over USB: turn on USB host on one unit (on the MIDI IN page). Connect the other unit to it through a powered hub. Keep Link on Auto on the two units: the unit that is the USB host leads. To set the lead by hand, use Lead or Follow.
  2. The units send a hello message about once a second. When each unit receives the other, both show LINKED.
  3. Press PLAY on the lead. The follower starts at the same time and uses the tempo of the lead (EXT).

While the units are linked:

  • The harmony is shared. The lead sends its key, scale and every chord change. The follower plays its own pad, bass, pluck and acid over the chords of the lead. Thus two different genres or pieces still play in the same key.
  • The sections are shared (Sections on Follow): when the lead plays a breakdown, build or drop, the follower plays it on the same bar. On Own, the follower keeps its own sections.
  • Plays splits the band. Each unit can leave parts to the other unit. Beat plays the drums and bass. Top plays the pad, pluck and acid. Drums plays only the drums. No drums plays the other parts. For example, set the lead to Beat and the follower to Top. Or set the two units to All for a thicker sound.
  • Neither sends its notes over the link. The units send only the clock and the link messages. Thus the follower does not play the notes of the lead as keys.
  • The LINK page shows the partner (PERF-1 FM-1 or PERF-1 SMK) and if this unit leads or follows. If it shows TWO LEADS: SET ONE or NO LEAD: SET ONE, set one unit to Lead (or Follow).

If the partner is gone for 3.5 seconds (disconnected or switched off), each unit shows PARTNER GONE: ALONE. It then continues alone, with its own chords and all its parts again. The link uses MIDI channel 16, CCs 20-31 (CCs are MIDI control messages). When Link is on, other equipment on your MIDI out receives a CC 20 on channel 16 about once a second. If this causes a problem, set Link to Off.

We tested the cable with an FM-1 as the lead and a PERF-1 SMK as the follower for eight minutes (2026-11-16). The units stayed linked all the time, all messages arrived, and there was no measurable load on either unit. We did not test the link over USB between two units yet. If the follower does not link or does not follow over USB, tell us. Each unit keeps its own mood, piece and (on Own) sections. The link shares the tempo, the harmony and which unit plays which parts.

15Your own DX7 voices

The DX7 was a classic FM synth from Yamaha. Thousands of its sounds (voices) are free on the internet as .syx files. PERF-1 can keep 16 of your own DX7 voices. It plays them on the pluck. They stay in memory when you switch off. On the Pluck page, they come after the 60 built-in DX voices. To select them, use the Sound knob, or SEL + ALGORITHM on a pluck page. Each voice shows as DX and the name of the voice (ten characters).

Loading them from the installer page

  1. Open the installer. Find Load DX7 voices.
  2. Choose one or more .syx files. A 32-voice bank, single voices or a bare 4096-byte bank all work. The page shows a list of the voices in them.
  3. Tick up to 16 voices. Press Send the chosen voices. The page clears the loaded voices and sends the new voices in order.

Clear the loaded voices empties the slots.

The FM-1 must run PERF-1 and must be connected. Use Chrome or Edge. When the browser asks, allow MIDI with SysEx (the kind of MIDI message that carries sound data). Make sure that nothing else uses the MIDI port of the FM-1. If the FM-1 does not answer, the page shows The FM-1 did not answer (an older firmware?).

Loading them from a MIDI librarian

You can also send a DX7 SysEx dump from any MIDI librarian app (an app that keeps and sends sounds) to the USB MIDI port of the FM-1.

  • A 32-voice bank replaces the loaded voices with its first 16.
  • A single voice goes after the last loaded voice. If all 16 slots are full, it replaces the last voice.

The FM-1 checks the two types against the DX7 checksum (a number that shows if the data is damaged). It rejects a damaged file. The header shows the result: DX7 VOICES 16 LOADED, 1 ADDED, CLEARED, BAD CHECKSUM or NOT TAKEN.

(For people who write their own tools: the page also uses its own message: F0 7D 50 56, slot, 128 bytes, checksum, F7. 7F alone clears. The FM-1 answers F0 7D 50 57, so a sender can wait for each voice.)

Good to know

  • The FM-1 saves the voices a couple of seconds after your last change (the music dips, as for all saves). The voices stay after power-off.
  • A voice plays as it was made: the algorithm, the envelopes (how the sound changes over time) and their scaling, velocity, LFO (a slow wave that moves the sound), pitch envelope and feedback. PERF-1 does not use the pitch bend range, mod wheel, portamento (a slide between notes) and mono mode (one note at a time) settings of the voice yet. The pitch bend and the mod wheel of a MIDI keyboard still work on a DX voice (section 14). To edit the voice itself, use the editor page (below).
  • Loaded voices are not level-matched (their levels are not made equal) to each other or to the other sounds. They play up to four notes at the same time. If a voice is too loud or too quiet, turn the Level of the pluck.
  • A piece keeps the sound of the pluck by slot number. For example, a piece uses your voice in slot 3, and then you load other voices. The piece now plays the voice that is in slot 3. The factory pieces never use your slots.
  • The Sound knob can stop on an empty slot. The screen shows it as (empty), and it makes no sound. SEL + ALGORITHM skips empty slots.

Editing them: the editor page

Use the editor to change sounds and DX7 voices from a computer. Use Chrome or Edge. Connect the FM-1 with USB. Allow MIDI with SysEx. Make sure that nothing else uses the MIDI port of the FM-1. The editor has two tabs.

Sound has all the settings of the piece, in groups by voice (Tempo & global, Pad, Bass, Acid, Pluck, Drums, Space & master, Mixer). It uses the names, ranges and values of the FM-1. A last page has the other settings: the level, tune, decay, probability and length of each drum lane; the pan and send of each layer; the noise; the octaves; and the by-hand switches.

When you move a control, it changes the piece that plays, the same as when you turn the knob. To keep the change, store the piece on the FM-1 itself (GLO page, SAVE). The page reads the values of the FM-1 when it connects. It reads them again when you press Read everything from the FM-1. Thus the page shows the values that the FM-1 has. The ↺ button puts a setting back to its default.

DX7 voices edits the 16 slots. Press Read the slots from the FM-1. Select a slot. Then edit:

  • the name of the voice
  • the algorithm (how the six operators connect together; the operators are the six sound sources of a DX7 voice). The page shows a drawing of all 32. It marks the operators that you hear (carriers), and a curl shows feedback. To edit an operator, click it in the drawing.
  • feedback, oscillator sync, transpose, the pitch envelope and the LFO
  • for each of the six operators: output level, ratio or fixed frequency (coarse, fine, detune), the four-stage envelope (with a graph), level scaling (break point, left and right depth and curve), rate scaling, amplitude-modulation sensitivity and velocity sensitivity.

When Live is on, each change goes to the slot immediately. Thus the pluck plays the voice that you edit. Choose it on the pluck sets the pluck to that slot. The slots fill in order. To add a voice, send it to the next free slot. Init voice starts from a plain sine wave.

Choose files loads .syx banks and single voices into a list. You can take voices from this list (click a voice to edit it). Save the slots as a bank and Save this voice write .syx files. Use them in a librarian, or keep them for later. The FM-1 saves the slots a couple of seconds after your last change.

16Load, telemetry and the logger

Read this section if the sound crackles or stutters, or if you want to report a problem. It explains the load numbers on the mix map. It also tells you how to record a status log to send with a bug report.

The load line on screen

Hold GLO to see the mix map. After a dropout, or while the FM-1 is easing off (it does less work to keep up), the top right shows a line like CPU 41 XR 0. Below it are C2 38 and S1, and below them is the phase of the auto-DJ. The help card of GLO always shows this line (hold GLO alone for about a second).

You seeIt means
CPU nnHow hard the FM-1 works to make the sound, in percent. The work to draw the screen does not count.
XR nThe number of dropouts. Each dropout is a click or a stutter, where a very small part of the sound plays again. If this number goes up, you heard a glitch (a short fault in the sound).
C2 nnThe second processor of the FM-1 helps, and it is nn % busy.
C1The second processor stopped after a fault. Everything runs on one processor until you switch off. Switch off and on again to get the second processor back.

S2The FM-1 eases off more. The DX voices are also limited to four notes at the same time.
S3The last step. The DX voices are limited to two notes, and the Echoplex and the vinyl noise are off. The output clip uses the version that needs less work.
QD nCommands that the sound engine did not get, because too many came at the same time (for example, a knob turned or a piece loaded). Nobody has seen this yet. If it shows, a setting possibly did not change, so tell us.

The FM-1 eases off one step when there is a dropout. It also eases off one step when about a quarter of its renders are late within ten seconds (a load that stays too high). A render is one calculation of a short part of the sound. A late render that loses sound counts as a dropout (0.17.56). A late render that does not lose sound causes no problem by itself.

S1 comes earlier, before any dropout: at 40 late renders in ten seconds. These late renders show that the margin is small. This step turns off only the enhancers, not the DX voices. The FM-1 goes back one step after twenty seconds without a dropout or a high load. With S1 to S3 on, the sound is a little thinner, but it is not broken.

The heaviest combinations are the DX plucks with the shimmer reverb, and the fast genres (dnb, breaks). If you hear crackles there, this is the probable cause.

There is always a delay of a few milliseconds between the time you press a key and the time you hear it. The DX plucks start very slightly later than the plain sounds.

Recording a status log in the browser

This is the easiest way to record what happens:

  1. Connect the FM-1 by USB. Then open the installer page.
  2. Find the Live status panel. Press Start. The page asks the FM-1 for its numbers four times a second. It shows the CPU, the second processor, the dropouts and the longest render against its budget (the time that the render has). It also shows the work that is shed (turned off) or moved to the other processor, the genre and piece, the flash writes and the frames drawn.
  3. Play the piece that crackles for about one minute.
  4. Press Save CSV to save the log as a file.
  5. Attach the file to a bug report. Include the piece, its sounds and the tempo (section 19 has the link).

Recording with the logger (for people comfortable with Python)

The PERF-1 source has a small Python tool. It records the same type of log from a computer.

  1. Install the software that the tool needs: pip install mido python-rtmidi.
  2. When the FM-1 is connected by USB, run python tools/fm1log.py. Use --list to see the MIDI ports. Use --port and --out to select a port and a file name.
  3. The tool writes telemetry/fm1_<time>.csv. Send that file.

python tools/fm1sum.py telemetry/<file>.csv prints a summary of the load, the dropouts and the stage times for each genre and piece. Do not use fm1log.py --bench while you make music. It is a development test, and it causes one glitch.

The FM-1 sends nothing until it gets a request. It stops ten seconds after the last request. If it is busy, it skips a reading and does not wait. Thus the logging cannot delay the music.

  • A fader or knob sets the value directly. The bottom of its travel is the lowest value of the setting, and the top is the highest value. The value jumps to the position of the knob. The FM-1 has no pick-up mode.

18Troubleshooting

No sound. Check the MASTER knob. Make sure that PLAY is lit. Then check these items:

  • The level of a layer can be at 0. Each voice page has its level on KNOB1, and the Mixer shows all the levels.
  • A voice marked BY HAND is silent until you play it. To give it back to the automatic part, push SEL + ENV on its page.
  • A track can be muted on the mix map (hold GLO to see it).
  • An effect latched (held on) in FX can make sounds silent. The header shows FX when an effect is latched.
  • On the Jam page, the voice that you play stops its own line while that page is open.
  • REMOTE makes the FM-1 silent on purpose (section 17).

A short dip or tick when you change a setting or store a piece. This is normal. While the FM-1 saves, the music fades out and then fades in again. The FM-1 saves settings a few seconds after your last change. Thus many fast changes cause only one dip.

A stored piece came back the way it was before. You stored it while it played, and the power went off before the save was complete. A store occurs immediately, with a short dip in the sound. Do not switch off during the dip.

Everything I saved is gone after I switch off, or an install stops immediately. Newer FM-1s (sold since autumn 2026) have a different memory chip. PERF-1 before 0.16.23, and many other firmwares, do not recognise this chip. They keep your saves only until power-off, and they cannot take an update (the installer says "nothing was written"). Nothing is broken.

Install 0.16.23 or newer. If the installer cannot connect to the FM-1, first put the original (stock) firmware back. Use a recovery tool such as FM-SWITCH, or the rescue page of the Firmware Hub. The Live status on the Install page shows which chip your FM-1 has (P25Q80 or UC25HQ80).

PERF-1 got heavier after I tried M-VAVE's V16 (CPU higher, easing off sooner). V16 also changes the startup code of the FM-1. With this code, the full FM-1 runs about 12% slower. If you install PERF-1 again, this does not change. On the Install page, Full speed checks your FM-1. It can put the V15 startup code back (it installs M-VAVE's V15, then PERF-1). FM-1s with the newer memory chip stay on V16 for now.

My changes are gone. The FM-1 keeps only a piece that you stored (Global, SAVE). If you did not store your changes, you lose them when you change the genre or switch off. See "What is remembered" in section 13.

The installer does not find the FM-1. Use Chrome or Edge. Use a USB data cable (some cables can only charge). Close all music software (DAW, a program to record and make music) that can use the MIDI port of the FM-1. When the page asks, allow MIDI with SysEx.

The installer offers an old version. Do a hard refresh of the page: Ctrl+F5, or Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac.

An install stopped before the end. Run it again. The FM-1 stays in update mode until an install is complete. If the FM-1 does not come back at all, see "Recovering an FM-1 that won't start" in section 1.

The screen says PERF-1 SAFE MODE. The FM-1 crashed two times during startup. Install again from the installer, or press PLAY to try again.

UPDATE MODE IN 3.. appeared. You held OCT- and OCT+ together. Release them before the countdown ends, and the FM-1 cancels the update mode.

The buttons or knobs do not match their labels. Do the hardware calibration (a Felucca feature). Hold OCT- and OCT+ while you switch the FM-1 on. The FM-1 asks you to press each button and turn each knob, one after the other. Then it saves the result. If you do not touch anything for 30 seconds, it stops and changes nothing.

The DX7 voices that I loaded do not show. Use the Sound knob of the pluck, or SEL + ALGORITHM on a Pluck page. Your voices come after the 60 built-in DX7 voices. Make sure that the page said that the voices were sent, and that the header showed DX7 VOICES n LOADED. Also make sure that the Level of the pluck is not 0. A bank must pass the DX7 checksum (a check that the data is correct).

Crackles or dropouts. Look at the load line (section 16). XR goes up by one for each click. S1 or S2 means that the FM-1 already eases off to keep up. Heavy DX plucks, a shimmer reverb and the fast genres cause the highest load. Record a status log for one minute (Live status on the installer page, or fm1log.py). Send the CSV to deamonSLINKY, with the piece, its sounds and the tempo.

The emulator does not see the FM-1. Close all DAWs that use the MIDI port of the FM-1. Use Chrome, Edge or Firefox (Safari does not support Web MIDI). If you want the panel of the FM-1 to play the emulator, the FM-1 must be in remote (SEL + GLO).

MIDI notes change my scale. Since 0.17.7, they change the scale only on the Global page, the same as the FM-1's own keys. On all other pages, they play a voice. On Global, send on a different channel, or set Notes on the MIDI IN page to Pads.
