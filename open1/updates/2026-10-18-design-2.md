The first video got more than 50 comments in a day. Thank you, they changed the design. Here's design 2:

![OPEN-1 design 2 next to the outline of design 1, at the same scale](media/open1-v2-compare.jpg)

**What changed**

- **27 keys instead of 37.** Lots of you said portability matters more than range, so OPEN-1 now has the same number of keys as the FM-1, with octave buttons for the rest. The body shrinks to about 320 × 230 mm, a third smaller and much squarer.
- **16 pads instead of 8.** A 4 × 4 grid: the layout people already have muscle memory for, and 16 steps for sequencing.
- **16 lit buttons instead of 14,** in two rows of 8. They have no printed names: each firmware labels them on the screen, so a button can be SAVE in one firmware and BUILD in another.
- **Push encoders.** All 8 encoders now click, so one of them can be the menu button.

Ten fewer keys and a smaller case should roughly pay for the extra pads and buttons, so the target price stays under $200. The number of RGB LEDs stays at 59.

**What stays, and why**

- **Silicone keys.** I read every comment asking for a better keybed. Silicone keys are a big part of keeping OPEN-1 cheap, and a low price is half the point of the project, so they stay. A premium version with a real keybed could come later if the first one does well.
- **The thumbstick,** not a touch pad. A stick is cheaper, sturdier, and simpler for firmware writers to use.

**Still on the table.** Tell me which of these you'd actually use: a sustain pedal jack, sync in and out, a switch for TRS MIDI type A or B, or CV and gate outputs for modular gear.

**How it compares to the SMK-37.** The M-VAVE SMK-37 is a great keyboard, and it runs some of the same custom firmware as the FM-1. But it has the same processor and memory limits as the FM-1. OPEN-1 is about headroom: about 75 times the working memory, audio in for sampling and effects, several firmwares stored at once, and a well-documented chip with a standard toolchain.
